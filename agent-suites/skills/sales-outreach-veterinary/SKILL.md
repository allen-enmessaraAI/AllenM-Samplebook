EMAIL RULES: Under 90 words. Mobile-first. Lead with a compliance metric or staffing pain signal. One ask only. Avoid anything that sounds like pharma or medical device sales — vets are deeply skeptical of vendor pitches.
CHANNEL PRIORITY: (1) Direct email to DVM, (2) Phone call to practice manager 9:30-11am, (3) LinkedIn for specialty/hospital directors, (4) VIN forum engagement for warm inbound (long play)
OUTREACH TIMING: Best days: Tuesday-Thursday. Best times: 7:00-8:00am (before first appointment) or 6:30-7:30pm (after last case). Avoid: Monday AM, holiday weeks, peak summer appointment season (June-August), VMX conference week (January — they're traveling or recovering).

## Phase 1: Veterinary-Specific Personalization Research

**CRITICAL:** Vet practices receive many vendor pitches (IDEXX, Zoetis, Merck, etc.). The only way to stand out is extreme specificity about their practice.

### 1.1 Veterinary Trigger Research

**Google Business Profile check (30 seconds):**
- Search "[practice name] veterinarian [city]"
- Note: Rating (under 4.2 = visible pain), review themes, response rate
- Reviews mentioning "3-week wait," "can't get an appointment," or "we were turned away" = scheduling/backlog pain — your opening line
- Reviews mentioning "staff was rushed" or "felt like a factory" = understaffed, burnout-driven culture

**Indeed job posting check (60 seconds):**
- Hiring "Veterinary Technician" with sign-on bonus → severe staffing shortage, workflow strain
- Hiring multiple open positions simultaneously → operational stress, potential burnout
- Hiring "Practice Manager" → owner overwhelmed with operations, wants to step back from admin
- Hiring "Client Service Representative" or "Receptionist" → front-of-house chaos

**Practice website check (2 minutes):**
- Count DVMs listed (2+ = right size)
- Check PIMS listed anywhere (job postings often name it: "experience with AVImark preferred")
- Look for online booking widget → tech-forward signal
- Check if they list Chewy or 1-800-PetMeds anywhere on their site (pharmacy leakage acceptance)
- Check "Services" page for specialist mix: dermatology, surgery, internal medicine → higher revenue, higher complexity

## Phase 3: The 5-Email Veterinary Sequence

### Veterinary Email Writing Rules

**Length:** Under 90 words for emails 1-3. Under 70 words for emails 4-5. DVMs read email on mobile between cases.

**Voice rules for veterinary:**
- Write like a peer in animal health, not a SaaS vendor
- Reference their patients (cats, dogs, pets) — it grounds the pitch in their world
- Never say "optimize," "leverage," "synergy," "streamline," or "scale"
- DO say: "heartworm compliance," "wellness visits," "recheck appointments," "client retention"
- Use their PIMS name if known: "in AVImark" not "in your system"

**Subject lines that work in veterinary:**
- "your heartworm compliance rate"
- "3 open vet tech positions"
- "[City] independent practice question"
- "what [Practice] in [City] found"
- "pharmacy revenue question"

## Phase 4: Phone Approach (Veterinary-Specific)

**When to call:** 9:30-11:00am Tuesday-Thursday (between morning appointments and lunch)
**Who to call first:** Practice Manager — higher answer rate than calling the DVM directly
**Script:**
```
"Hi — this is [name] with [company]. I'm not pitching anything today.
I sent Dr. [name] a quick note about compliance gaps I've been seeing
in [city] practices, and I wanted to make sure it didn't disappear
into spam.

Could you let [him/her] know I called? Or if there's a better email
address for vendor correspondence, I can use that instead.

I'll keep it to 10 minutes when we talk — promise."
```

**Avoid:** Calling during lunchtime (12-1pm), during afternoon rush (3-5pm when sick patients are coming in), Friday afternoons.
