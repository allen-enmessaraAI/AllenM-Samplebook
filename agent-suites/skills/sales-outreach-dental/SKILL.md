EMAIL RULES: Under 80 words. Mobile-first (dentists read email on iPhone between patients). No jargon. Lead with a specific production metric. One ask only.

## Phase 1: Dental-Specific Personalization Research

**CRITICAL:** Never send a generic dental pitch. Every email must reference something specific about their practice.

### 1.1 Dental Trigger Research

Before writing a single word, run these searches and checks:

**Google Business Profile check (30 seconds):**
- Search "[practice name] [city] dentist" on Google Maps
- Note: Star rating (under 4.3 = reputation pain), review count, response rate to reviews, most recent negative review theme
- A review mentioning "scheduling issues," "hard to get an appointment," or "billing confusion" is your opening line

**Indeed job posting check (60 seconds):**
- Search "[practice name]" on Indeed.com
- Actively hiring "Scheduling Coordinator" → no-show/scheduling pain
- Actively hiring "Treatment Coordinator" → low treatment acceptance pain
- Actively hiring "Insurance Coordinator" or "Dental Biller" → billing chaos
- Multiple open positions → staffing crisis = operational pain

**Practice website check (2 minutes):**
- Count operatories listed (more = higher production potential)
- Note technology listed: iTero, CBCT, CEREC, same-day crowns → capital investment appetite
- Check team page for new associate dentist → growth mode, more production pressure
- Look for "Now Accepting New Patients" → new patient hunger
- Check if they list Dentrix/Eaglesoft/Open Dental anywhere (PMS identification)

## Phase 3: The 5-Email Dental Sequence

### Dental Email Writing Rules (Override Base Rules)

**Dental-specific length rule:** Under 80 words for emails 1-4. Under 60 words for email 5. Dentists read email on their phone between patients — if it doesn't fit on one screen, it won't get read.

**Subject line rules for dental:**
- 4-6 words maximum
- Include a number when possible ("12 hygiene patients last month")
- No exclamation marks
- No "free" or "schedule a demo"
- Examples that work: "your hygiene column," "340 overdue patients," "Dr. [name] in [city]," "Eaglesoft question"

**Opening line rules for dental:**
- NEVER: "I hope you're well," "My name is X," "I'm reaching out about..."
- ALWAYS: Start with a specific observation about their practice from your research
- Reference their Google rating, a recent hire, a technology investment, or a specific number

## Phase 4: Phone Call Approach (Dental-Specific)

Cold email alone has lower response rates with dental than with other verticals. Include phone touchpoints:

**When to call:** 9:30-11:00am Tuesday-Thursday (before lunch, after morning patients check in)
**Who to call:** Office Manager or front desk — NOT the doctor directly
**Goal:** Get the office manager to relay your message or book 10 minutes with the doctor

**Phone script for OM:**
```
"Hi, this is [name] with [company]. I'm not trying to sell anything
today — I just sent Dr. [name] a quick note about hygiene production
gaps I've been seeing in [city] practices, and I wanted to make sure
it didn't get buried in the email inbox.

Could you let [him/her] know I called? Or if there's a better email
address for [his/her] clinical correspondence, I can send there.

I'll be quick — promise."
```

**Best practices:**
- If the OM answers, treat her as a champion not a gatekeeper
- Ask: "Are you the right person to talk to about patient scheduling and recall?" (Often, yes)
- Never call during 12-1pm (lunch) or after 4:30pm (end-of-day chaos)

## Phase 5: LinkedIn Touchpoints (Dental-Specific)

LinkedIn is most effective for **DSO contacts** (Regional Directors, VP Operations) — not solo-practice dentists who are rarely active on LinkedIn.

**For DSO contacts:**
- Day 0: Connection request — "Saw [DSO group] is expanding in [region]. I work with regional dental groups on production gap analysis — thought it'd be good to connect."
- Day 5: Comment on a recent post about DSO operations or dental industry trends
- Day 10: LinkedIn message — "Hey [name], sent you an email about production metrics for multi-site groups — wanted to connect here too. Happy to share what similar-size groups are seeing."

**For solo/small group dentists:**
- Skip LinkedIn. Email + phone + direct mail are far more effective.

## Phase 6: Direct Mail (Dental-Specific Tactic)

Unlike most B2B markets, direct mail works well in dental. Consider adding one physical mail touchpoint:

**Best practices:**
- Send between Day 3-7 (after email 1-2 have been sent)
- Hand-address the envelope — it gets opened
- One-page letter, not a brochure
- Reference a specific metric for their practice size
- Include a QR code linking to a demo or case study
- Handwritten P.S. gets read more than the body: "P.S. — Dentrix practices in [state] are averaging $14,200 in recovered hygiene production in the first 90 days."
