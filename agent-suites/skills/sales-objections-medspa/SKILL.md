### Objection 16: "Allergan already handles our marketing through Alle / Brilliant Distinctions"

**What it really means:** The practice has a relationship with Allergan (the maker of Botox/Juvederm) through their Alle loyalty program and receives co-marketing support. They're confusing Allergan's brand loyalty program with a client retention system.

**FFR Response:**
"Allergan's Alle program is a real asset — it gives your clients a reason to stay loyal to Botox and Juvederm specifically. [Med spa in similar market] felt the same way — they were in the Alle program, getting co-op marketing dollars, and thought they had retention covered. What they found was that Alle tracks loyalty to Allergan's brands, not loyalty to their practice. When a client moved to another practice that also offered Botox, the Alle points moved with them. Their rebooking rate for first-time clients was 44% — the Alle program wasn't preventing them from going to a competitor, it was just making it easier to go to any Allergan provider. We track loyalty to YOUR practice — the relationship, not the brand."

**ABC Response:**
"Alle is a solid tool and co-op marketing dollars are real money — I'm glad you're using it. (Acknowledge) Here's the distinction though: Alle builds loyalty to Allergan products, and any Allergan provider can benefit from it. What you want is clients who are loyal to YOUR practice — your injectors, your experience, your brand — not just whoever sells Botox nearest to them. (Bridge) Is your current rebooking rate for first-time clients above 70%? If it is, Alle might be doing the job. If it's below 60%, there's a gap that Alle isn't closing because it's not designed to. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "What's your current rebooking rate for first-time clients specifically — not just active clients overall, but the percentage of first-timers who come back within 6 months?"

**Proof point:** "[Named practice] was in the Alle program and had a 46% first-visit rebooking rate. After 90 days with us, their rebooking rate for first-time clients hit 68%. The Alle program didn't change — they kept running it. What changed was the systematic follow-up for clients who got the Alle email but didn't rebook."

**When to walk away:** If they have an exclusive marketing agreement with Allergan that financially incentivizes them to use only Allergan-provided tools (rare but does happen in high-spend practices) — document and revisit at contract renewal.

### Objection 17: "Our booking software (Boulevard / Zenoti / Mindbody) already does retention"

**What it really means:** They're paying for a sophisticated practice management platform and believe the built-in communication features cover their retention needs. They don't want to add a point solution on top of their existing tech investment.

**FFR Response:**
"Boulevard and Zenoti are excellent platforms — they're genuinely the best in class for med spa booking and operations. [Practice on Zenoti] felt the same way — they'd invested in Zenoti specifically because of its retention features. What they found was that Zenoti's automated messages fire well for the first reminder and post-visit follow-up, but the exception workflow — what happens when a client doesn't respond to the 3-month Botox reminder, or when a consultation didn't convert and nobody followed up — that required manual action that the front desk wasn't consistently executing. We handle the exception workflow as an overlay — everything that needs a human touch but consistently slips through."

**ABC Response:**
"Boulevard is a great choice — I work with a lot of practices on that platform and the booking experience is excellent. (Acknowledge) Here's the gap I see in most practices using Boulevard or Zenoti for retention: the platform sends the automated messages, but there's no intelligent follow-up logic for the clients who don't respond. The lapsed client at 5 months, the consultation that didn't book, the client whose last appointment was 8 months ago — those exceptions pile up and turn into revenue your front desk means to follow up on but doesn't. (Bridge) What's your consultation-to-booking conversion rate right now? And what percentage of your clients return within 90 days of their last visit? Those two numbers tell me pretty quickly whether this is a gap for you. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Which retention features inside Boulevard/Zenoti are you actively using — automated reminders, email campaigns, both? And who on your team is responsible for following up on clients who don't respond to the automated messages?"

**When to walk away:** If they have a dedicated patient coordinator who manually works the exception list every day, and their rebooking rate is above 72%, their platform + human system is working. Revisit at their platform contract renewal with a new ROI angle.

### Objection 18: "My injectors bring in their own clients — I don't need help with retention"

**What it really means:** The practice relies on individual injectors who have personal followings and book their own clients. The owner may not fully understand the practice-level retention risk this creates — if an injector leaves, their clients leave with them.

**FFR Response:**
"Having injectors with personal followings is a real asset — it means your team has genuine skill and client relationships. [Med spa owner] said the same thing — two of her three injectors had strong personal books. What she found was that when her top injector left to open her own practice, 60% of that injector's clients followed her out the door. The practice lost $18,000 in monthly revenue in 90 days. What we do is build client loyalty to your practice brand, not just to the individual injector — so when a client books with [Injector Name], they also know Dr. Smith's practice, receive communications from the practice, and have a reason to stay even if the injector changes."

**ABC Response:**
"Injectors with strong personal followings are a competitive advantage — I wouldn't change that. (Acknowledge) Here's the risk I want to flag though: when client loyalty lives in the injector's relationship rather than your practice's brand, your revenue walks out the door if that injector leaves. And in this market, experienced injectors have a lot of options. (Bridge) What percentage of your clients would you estimate are loyal to a specific injector vs. loyal to your practice? And if your top injector left tomorrow, how much of their book do you think would stay? That's the retention gap we help close — without changing anything about how your injectors work. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "If your top injector left and opened a practice 5 miles away, what percentage of their clients do you think would follow them? That number tells us the practice-level retention risk."

**When to walk away:** If the practice is set up specifically as an injector-employment model where the injectors have contractual rights to their client lists (common in some suite rental and 1099 contractor arrangements) — you need to restructure how you frame the value or revisit when a non-compete issue arises.

### Objection 19: "We don't have time to onboard anything — we're fully booked and short-staffed"

**What it really means:** The practice is running hot and the idea of any additional work feels impossible. This is an emotional objection from an overwhelmed operator — the same logic that prevents them from getting out of the hamster wheel.

**FFR Response:**
"I hear that — when you're running fully booked and short-staffed, the last thing you need is a vendor asking for your time. [Med spa owner] was in exactly this position — she was injecting 9 hours a day, her patient coordinator had just quit, and she hadn't taken a day off in three months. What she found was that the 4 hours she invested in onboarding saved her front desk 90 minutes every day in manual follow-up — within 6 weeks they had a 12% improvement in rebooking rate and she told me it was the first time in months the practice felt like it was running itself. The busiest practices are the ones where automation pays off fastest because the manual gap is biggest."

**ABC Response:**
"Fully booked and short-staffed tells me two things: your practice is in demand, and your team is maxed out. (Acknowledge) Here's what I know about practices in that situation — the work that doesn't get done consistently is the client follow-up: the post-consultation reach-out, the 3-month Botox reminder follow-up, the lapsed client at 7 months. Those slip because urgent things always displace them. We automate those exact tasks. Your team doesn't take on more work — they do less of the repetitive manual stuff. (Bridge) The onboarding requires 4 hours from your practice manager, then it runs. Could we find 4 hours in the next 30 days? If not, I understand — let's set a date to reconnect when things calm down. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Who on your team currently handles client follow-up and rebooking outreach — is it your front desk, a dedicated patient coordinator, or does it fall through the cracks when you're this busy?"

**When to walk away:** If they genuinely have no operations staff (solo owner doing everything herself with zero admin support) and they are in active crisis, it's not the right time. Come back in 60 days with a concrete time-savings offer.

### Objection 20: "We mainly get clients from Instagram — social handles our marketing"

**What it really means:** The practice relies heavily on Instagram for new client acquisition and has conflated acquisition with retention. They don't necessarily have a retention problem in their mind — but they almost certainly do in reality.

**FFR Response:**
"Instagram is a powerful acquisition channel for aesthetics — you're right. [Med spa with strong Instagram] was generating 40+ leads a month from Instagram and felt like marketing was handled. What they found when we dug into their numbers was that only 31% of those Instagram-acquired clients came back for a second visit. They were spending $4,500/month on Instagram content and paid ads to keep filling the top of funnel — and losing most of those clients after the first visit. We helped them build a systematic post-visit follow-up sequence. Their rebooking rate for Instagram-acquired clients went from 31% to 58% in 90 days. Same acquisition spend — dramatically higher revenue per client."

**ABC Response:**
"Instagram is genuinely the best acquisition channel in aesthetics — I wouldn't change that for you. (Acknowledge) Here's the question though: what happens after a client comes in from Instagram? If they have an amazing experience but no systematic follow-up — no 'it's time for your 3-month touch-up' message, no 'you have a VIP credit about to expire' — a significant percentage of them won't come back unless they see another Instagram post that reminds them. (Bridge) What's your rebooking rate for first-time clients from Instagram specifically? I'll bet it's lower than your rebooking rate from referrals or returning clients. That gap is the retention problem underneath the acquisition success. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Do you track where your clients come from — Instagram vs. referral vs. Google — and do you know the rebooking rate for each source? Those numbers tell you a lot about where your retention gaps are."

**When to walk away:** If their Instagram funnel is genuinely producing high rebooking rates (above 65% for first-time clients) and they have a tight, simple service menu — this may not be a significant pain point. Shift the conversation to consultation conversion or average ticket instead.

### Objection 21: "We run promotions when we need more clients — we don't need a system for this"

**What it really means:** The practice is discount-dependent — they rely on flash sales, Groupon-style offers, or periodic promotions to drive revenue when it dips. This is a significant operational pattern that indicates retention is weak and they're treating symptoms rather than root causes.

**FFR Response:**
"Running promotions is a quick way to fill the schedule, and I completely understand the appeal. [Med spa similar in size] was doing the same thing — a 20% off neurotoxin promotion every time things slowed down. What they found was that their promo clients had a 22% rebooking rate at full price, vs. 71% for their regular-price clients. Every promotion was filling the schedule with clients who expected discounts and didn't stick around. After building a systematic retention program that kept their full-price clients coming back, they ran 40% fewer promotions in a year and actually grew revenue by 18%. They stopped filling holes — and the holes stopped appearing."

**ABC Response:**
"Promotions work for a quick revenue boost, no question about it. (Acknowledge) Here's what I'd challenge you to look at though: what's the rebooking rate for clients who came in through a promo vs. clients who booked at full price? In almost every practice I've analyzed, promo clients have a rebooking rate that's 20-40% lower than full-price clients. If that's true for your practice, every promotion is filling your schedule with lower-value, lower-loyalty clients — and making it harder to charge full price because your client base is trained to wait for deals. (Bridge) Would you be open to looking at that number together? If your promo clients rebook at the same rate as full-price clients, I'll tell you honestly that you probably have this handled. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Do you track the rebooking rate for clients who come in through a promotion vs. clients who book at full price? That's usually the most revealing number in this conversation."

**When to walk away:** If the practice is in financial distress and genuinely needs the short-term revenue from promotions to keep the doors open — don't try to sell them on a long-term retention play. They need cash now. Come back when their financial position stabilizes.

## Competitive Objections — Med Spa Market

### "Why you over Alle / Brilliant Distinctions?"
**One-sentence positioning:** "Alle builds loyalty to Allergan brands — we build loyalty to your practice."
**Response:** "Alle is a manufacturer loyalty program — it's excellent for keeping clients on Botox and Juvederm rather than switching to a competitor's toxin. We build loyalty to your practice specifically, so clients return to YOUR injectors regardless of which brand they prefer."
**Landmine questions:** "If a client's Alle points transferred to another Allergan provider in your area, would you lose them?" "What percentage of your clients are loyal to your practice vs. loyal to a specific injector?" "What's your rebooking rate for clients who aren't enrolled in Alle?"

### "Why you over Boulevard/Zenoti retention features?"
**One-sentence positioning:** "Boulevard sends the message — we execute the follow-up when the message doesn't convert."
**Response:** "Boulevard is excellent at scheduled automations — the post-visit thank you, the 3-month reminder. We handle the exception workflow: the client who didn't respond to the 3-month reminder, the consultation that didn't convert, the lapsed client at month 7. That logic requires more than a scheduled message."
**Landmine questions:** "When a client doesn't respond to your automated Boulevard reminder, what's the next step?" "Who on your team works the lapsed client list?" "What's your consultation-to-booking conversion rate?"

## Pricing Deep Dive — Med Spa Framing

**The Rebooking Revenue Frame:**
"Let's calculate your retention gap. Tell me your monthly new client count and average ticket. [Example: 80 new clients/month × $450 average ticket × 45% rebooking rate = 36 return visits × $450 = $16,200 in month-2 revenue. At 65% rebooking rate, that's $23,400. The gap is $7,200/month.] Our cost is $[X]/month. The payback period is less than a week of recovered clients."

**The Consultation Conversion Frame:**
"If your consultation conversion rate is 50% and you run 60 consultations per month, you're booking 30 clients. At 65% conversion, that's 39. The difference is 9 additional bookings per month × your average treatment value. What does that number look like? Our cost covers itself in less than 2 additional booked consultations per month."

**The Promo Dependency Frame:**
"How much do you spend on promotions each year — across discounts, ad spend, and team time? If we can move your rebooking rate up by 15%, you'll need 30-40% fewer promotions to maintain the same revenue. In most practices, the savings on promotional spend alone covers our cost."
