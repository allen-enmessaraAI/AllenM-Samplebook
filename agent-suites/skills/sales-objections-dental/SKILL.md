# Vertical: Dental / Oral Health

## Vertical Context

VERTICAL: Dental Practices
BUYER TITLES: Practice Owner (DDS/DMD), Office Manager, DSO Regional Director
TERMINOLOGY TO USE: operatory, hygiene column, production, collections, recare, recall, unscheduled treatment, PPO write-off, new patient flow, treatment acceptance rate, front desk, PMS (Dentrix/Eaglesoft/Open Dental)
PROOF POINT FORMAT: Always express ROI in dollars per day, per operatory, or per month — not percentages alone. Dentists think in daily production numbers.
TRUST SIGNALS: Peer DDS referral is #1. Named practice examples in their region > national case studies. Integration with their specific PMS is a hard trust filter.
COMPETITIVE CONTEXT: Most common incumbents are Weave (patient communication), Lighthouse 360/Solutionreach (recall), Dental Intel (analytics), RevenueWell (marketing). The "we already have Weave" objection is the most common in the market.

### Objection 16: "We already have Weave" (or Lighthouse / Solutionreach / Dentrix reminders)

**What it really means:** They've already bought a patient communication tool and don't want to pay for another. They may or may not be getting value from it — most practices have Weave turned on but underutilized.

**FFR Response:**
"I hear that a lot — Weave is everywhere in dental. [Dental practice in their market] had Weave too. In fact, they'd had it for two years. What they found was that the automated recall was running, but their hygiene reactivation rate was still sitting at 38% — below the industry benchmark of 55%. They weren't losing because of Weave, they were losing because of the 30% of overdue patients that Weave was tagging but nobody was actually following up on. We closed that gap and they recovered $14,000 in hygiene production in the first 60 days. Are you seeing your hygiene utilization numbers where you want them to be?"

**ABC Response:**
"Good — Weave is a solid tool and I'm glad you have it in place. (Acknowledge) Here's what I've seen with most Weave practices though: the reminder system fires, but the exception workflow — patients who don't respond, who cancel last-minute, who have unscheduled treatment sitting in the chart — that follow-up falls back to the front desk. And front desks in busy practices can't execute that consistently. (Bridge) Is that a gap you're experiencing? If your recare rate is above 65% and you have zero holes in the hygiene schedule, I'll be honest with you and say you probably don't need us. But most offices I talk to aren't there yet — what are your hygiene column numbers looking like? (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Which parts of Weave are you actually using — just reminders, or also the reactivation campaigns? And what's your current hygiene reactivation rate for patients who are 6+ months overdue?"

**Proof point:** "[Named practice] had Weave and still had 340 patients overdue for hygiene. We ran a targeted reactivation campaign on that specific list — they booked 47 appointments in 30 days, adding $21,000 in hygiene production."

**When to walk away:** If they're genuinely using Weave's full feature set including reactivation campaigns, have hygiene utilization above 70%, and their recall gap is under 15% — they may actually be well-served. Be honest and come back when their Weave contract is up for renewal.

### Objection 17: "My office manager handles all of this already"

**What it really means:** The owner trusts their OM completely and doesn't want to imply she's doing a bad job. This is also often used as a redirect — they want you to talk to the OM instead of continuing the conversation.

**FFR Response:**
"She sounds like a great office manager — the practices that have strong OMs are the ones that can actually implement something like this effectively. [Similar practice] said the same thing — their OM was excellent. What they found was that their OM was doing five different jobs at once, and the tasks that required consistent daily follow-up — reactivation calls, unscheduled treatment follow-up — were the ones that consistently fell through the cracks because urgent things always take priority. We gave their OM back 90 minutes per day by automating those repetitive touchpoints. She was actually the biggest champion for the tool because it freed her up to do the higher-value work she was already good at."

**ABC Response:**
"That's a real asset — practices with strong OMs have a huge operational advantage. (Acknowledge) I'm not here to replace what she's doing. What I find in most practices is that the OM's time is already maxed out on the things that can't be automated — handling patient escalations, managing staff scheduling, running reports for the doctor. The tasks we automate are the ones that should be systemized anyway — recall outreach, appointment fill-in, unscheduled treatment follow-up. (Bridge) Would it make sense to bring her into the conversation? If I can show you both how this fits around what she's already doing — not on top of it — I think she'd actually appreciate it. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "What does your OM spend the most time on every day? I want to make sure what we do complements that rather than adds to her plate."

**Proof point:** "The most common thing we hear from office managers after 90 days is 'I wish we'd done this sooner.' The tools that work best in dental are the ones that remove work from the front desk — not add to it."

**When to walk away:** If the office manager is the actual decision-maker and she's resistant (not just cautious), you need her as a champion. If she won't engage after two attempts, the deal likely isn't going anywhere without the dentist directly involved.

### Objection 18: "We can't afford to take our front desk away from the phones / We're too busy"

**What it really means:** They're understaffed or overscheduled and the idea of implementing anything new feels like one more burden. This is an emotional objection — they're overwhelmed and protecting their team.

**FFR Response:**
"I completely get that — a busy front desk is already stretched thin, and the last thing you need is something that takes more of their time. [Similar practice] said word-for-word the same thing when I first talked to them. They were a 6-operatory practice with a two-person front desk and a 50-patient day. What they found was that implementing took 4 hours total — split across two sessions — and then the front desk was actually fielding 30% fewer inbound calls because patients were getting proactive outreach before they needed to call in. Their front desk lead told me it was the first time in years she felt like she wasn't just reacting all day."

**ABC Response:**
"If your front desk is already maxed out, the last thing I want to do is add to their workload — I'm glad you said that because it tells me we're probably a fit. (Acknowledge) Here's the thing: the practices that are most overwhelmed at the front desk are usually the ones where the most manual, repetitive work is happening. Recall calls, appointment reminders, unscheduled treatment follow-up — those are the exact tasks we replace with automation. Your front desk doesn't have to do more — they actually do less of the reactive, repetitive stuff. (Bridge) Let me show you what the implementation actually requires from your team — it's 3 hours total, and your OM controls everything from one screen. If that still sounds like too much, I'll be honest about it. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "What are your front desk team members spending most of their time on right now — is it mostly incoming calls, scheduling, insurance, or something else?"

**Proof point:** "Practices our size typically see their inbound call volume drop 20-30% within 60 days of going live, because patients are getting proactive outreach before they have to call in."

**When to walk away:** If the practice is genuinely in crisis mode — multiple staff vacancies, a doctor out on leave, a billing backlog — this is not the time. Offer to reconnect in 60 days when things stabilize, set a specific date, and follow up.

### Objection 19: "We've been burned by a dental software company before"

**What it really means:** A previous vendor over-promised on integration with their PMS, the onboarding was a disaster, or the tool was abandoned because adoption failed. This is a trust objection rooted in past trauma.

**FFR Response:**
"I appreciate you sharing that — it tells me I need to earn your trust rather than just assume I have it. [Practice] said almost exactly the same thing — they'd tried a recall platform two years earlier that promised Eaglesoft integration and the sync broke within the first week. What they found when they worked with us was that we actually built our integration directly with the Eaglesoft API team — not a workaround — and we don't go live until we've verified that your specific version of Eaglesoft is syncing correctly. They went live, it worked, and they've been running for 18 months without a single sync issue. Would it help if I put you in touch with them directly before you made any decision?"

**ABC Response:**
"That's really important for me to hear — and I don't want to repeat whatever happened. (Acknowledge) Can you tell me specifically what broke down? Was it the PMS integration, the onboarding process, the ongoing support, or the actual results? Because if I know what went wrong, I can tell you honestly whether we have the same problem or a different approach. (Bridge) I'd rather you disqualify us upfront if we have the same issue than have you go through that experience again. If it's something we've solved, I can prove it — specifically, not with a generic claim. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "What specifically went wrong — was it the integration with your practice management software, the onboarding, or the tool itself not working as promised?"

**Proof point:** Have a technical integration document for their specific PMS version ready to share. Offer a live 15-minute demo showing real-time sync with their PMS version — not a recording.

**When to walk away:** If the previous experience was traumatic enough that they're completely closed to any new vendor in this category regardless of evidence — and this comes across as a firm "we're never doing this again" — honor it. Stay in touch, check in in 6 months, and let time rebuild trust.

### Objection 20: "HIPAA — how do you handle patient data?"

**What it really means:** This is a legitimate and important concern, not a brush-off. Dental practices are HIPAA-covered entities and their Office Manager or doctor has been burned by compliance issues before, or they're appropriately cautious.

**FFR Response:**
"That's exactly the right question to ask, and I'd be concerned about any vendor that didn't have a clear answer. [Multi-location dental group] had the same question — their compliance officer asked for documentation before they'd even take a demo. What they found was that we have a signed Business Associate Agreement ready on day one, all data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and we don't share or sell patient data under any circumstances. I'll send you our full HIPAA compliance documentation and BAA template before our next conversation. Would that be the right thing to share with your compliance person?"

**ABC Response:**
"That's the first question I'd want answered too — protecting patient data is non-negotiable. (Acknowledge) Here's where we stand: we operate as a Business Associate under HIPAA, we execute a BAA with every practice before any data touches our system, we are SOC 2 Type II certified, all PHI is encrypted at rest and in transit, and we have never had a reportable breach. (Bridge) I'll send you our full compliance documentation right after this call — our legal team can also get on a call with your compliance person or attorney if that would be helpful. Is there a specific aspect of HIPAA compliance you want to make sure we address? (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Who at your practice is responsible for HIPAA compliance — is that you, your office manager, or do you work with a third-party compliance consultant? I want to make sure I get the documentation to the right person."

**Proof point:** Have your BAA template, SOC 2 certification, and a one-page HIPAA compliance summary ready to send immediately after this objection is raised.

**When to walk away:** Never walk away from a HIPAA objection — take it seriously and answer it thoroughly. If they want a legal review before proceeding, facilitate it. If after a full compliance review they determine you don't meet their standard — and you genuinely don't — be honest and don't try to force the deal.

### Objection 21: "We're looking at joining a DSO — we don't want to sign any contracts right now"

**What it really means:** The practice owner is in active conversations with DSO acquisition groups (Heartland, Aspen, Pacific Dental Services, etc.) and doesn't want to take on commitments that could complicate the deal. This is a timing and transition objection.

**FFR Response:**
"That makes complete sense — I wouldn't want to complicate your options either. A couple of practices I've worked with were in the same evaluation phase. What they found was interesting: practices with documented systems and technology in place actually commanded higher valuations in DSO negotiations because it demonstrates operational maturity. Two of them ended up signing with DSOs that kept the tools in place because the systems were already working. Would it be worth a quick conversation about what DSOs typically look for in practice operations? Even if timing doesn't work now, I can at least share what's helped other practices in that process."

**ABC Response:**
"Totally understand — and I don't want to be the thing that complicates your evaluation. (Acknowledge) Here's one thing worth knowing though: DSOs do due diligence on practice systems and operational metrics. Practices with strong recall rates, hygiene utilization above 60%, and documented follow-up processes typically get better offers and smoother transitions. If the DSO conversation takes 6-12 months, that's actually a window where improving your metrics could help your position. (Bridge) Would it be helpful to talk about what DSO buyers look at operationally, even if we don't move forward on anything right now? I'm happy to give you a perspective that has nothing to do with selling you something. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Are you actively in conversations with a specific DSO group, or still exploring options? And is there a specific timeline you're working toward?"

**When to walk away:** If they have a signed LOI with a DSO already — genuinely walk away and come back after the transition closes. Set a 6-month calendar reminder. DSO-owned practices often have different vendor evaluations post-acquisition.

### Objection 22: "I need to check with my Henry Schein rep / Patterson rep first"

**What it really means:** The practice has a deep relationship with their dental supply dealer (Henry Schein, Patterson, Benco) and uses that rep as a trusted advisor for technology recommendations. They won't buy anything without that vetting.

**FFR Response:**
"That makes a lot of sense — your Henry Schein rep knows your practice and you trust their judgment. [Similar practice] ran everything through their Patterson rep too. What they found was that their rep was actually supportive because we don't compete with what Henry Schein sells — we complement it. In fact, we work with several Henry Schein teams who recommend us specifically because it helps their practices grow, which grows their supply orders too. I'd be happy to put together a one-pager for your rep that explains exactly how we fit alongside the rest of your tech stack — would that be useful?"

**ABC Response:**
"That's a smart way to evaluate things — your dealer rep has context on your practice that I don't have yet. (Acknowledge) I'll be honest: most of what we do doesn't overlap with what Henry Schein or Patterson offer, so there shouldn't be any conflict. But if it would give you more confidence, I'm happy to get on a call with you and your rep together. (Bridge) Would your rep be open to a 20-minute call? If they have concerns, I'd rather address them directly. (Close)"

**Follow-up question:** "Is your Henry Schein/Patterson rep typically involved in technology decisions like this, or more on the supply side? I want to make sure I'm reaching the right person."

**When to walk away:** If the dental dealer has an exclusive preferred vendor relationship in your category and is actively recommending a competitor — and the practice won't consider anything outside that vendor list — it's a structural barrier. Focus on other prospects and check back when the dealer relationship or preferred vendor status changes.

## Competitive Objections — Dental Market

### "Why you over Weave?"
**One-sentence positioning:** "Weave keeps patients from cancelling — we recover the ones who already have."
**Response:** "Weave is built for communication — reminders, two-way texting, phones. What we do is revenue recovery — specifically reactivation of overdue hygiene patients and unscheduled treatment follow-up. They're complementary, not competing. Most of our best practices run both."
**Landmine questions:** "What's your hygiene reactivation rate for patients 6+ months overdue?" "How many open treatment plans are sitting in unscheduled status right now?" "Is anyone actually working that list, or does it sit in Weave's dashboard?"

### "Why you over Lighthouse 360 / Solutionreach / Demandforce?"
**One-sentence positioning:** "Those are recall reminder platforms — we're a production recovery engine."
**Response:** "Lighthouse and Solutionreach send the reminders. The question is what happens when a patient doesn't respond, or when they cancel at 7am and there's a hole in the schedule. That exception workflow is where production leaks. We close that gap."
**Landmine questions:** "When a hygiene appointment cancels same-day, what's your fill-in process right now?" "What percentage of patients who get recalled actually book?"

### "Why you over Dental Intel?"
**One-sentence positioning:** "Dental Intel shows you the problem — we fix it automatically."
**Response:** "Dental Intel is an analytics platform — it tells you that you have $80K in unscheduled treatment and 200 overdue hygiene patients. We automate the outreach to actually book those patients. Think of it as the engine under the dashboard."
**Landmine questions:** "You're using Dental Intel — are you actually running the reactivation campaigns from inside it, or is that still a manual process?" "Who's executing on the insights Dental Intel surfaces?"

## Pricing Deep Dive — Dental Framing

When handling price objections in dental, always anchor to production math:

**The Production Math Frame:**
"Let's do the math specific to your practice. Your average hygiene appointment is probably $150-$250 in production. If we help you recover 10 overdue hygiene patients per month — which is conservative — that's $1,500-$2,500 in additional monthly production. Our investment is $[X]/month. That's a payback period of [X] days from the first recovered hygiene appointment."

**The Daily Operatory Frame:**
"Think of it this way: an open operatory hour costs you $150-$400 in lost production. Our tool is designed to fill those holes before they happen. If we prevent even 3 cancellations from becoming no-shows per month, we've more than paid for ourselves."

**The Unscheduled Treatment Frame:**
"The average practice has $50,000-$150,000 in diagnosed but unscheduled treatment sitting in their charts right now. We automate outreach to that specific list. If we help you convert 5% of that — one patient a week — the ROI is immediate."
