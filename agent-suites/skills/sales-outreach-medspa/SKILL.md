## Phase 1: Med Spa-Specific Personalization Research

**CRITICAL:** Med spa owners receive dozens of vendor emails per week. The only emails that get opened are ones that reference their specific practice by name or their specific numbers.

### 1.1 Med Spa Trigger Research

**Facebook Ad Library check (2 minutes — free):**
- Go to facebook.com/ads/library and search "[practice name]"
- If they're running discount promotions ("20% off Botox," "Buy 2 get 1 free filler") → discount dependency = retention problem. This is your most powerful trigger.
- If they're running new patient acquisition ads but no retention content → acquisition-heavy, retention gap
- Frequency of ads: running 5+ ads simultaneously → big marketing spend but unclear ROI

**Instagram check (2 minutes):**
- Search "[practice name]" on Instagram
- Follower count vs. engagement rate: 10K followers with 40 likes per post → low engagement = reach problem or content-to-conversion gap
- Post content: majority promotional → discount-dependent
- Last before/after posted: weeks ago → photo management issue
- Bio: no booking link or outdated → front-end friction

**Google Business Profile check:**
- Rating under 4.3 → visible quality or communication issue
- Reviews mentioning "couldn't get a callback," "hard to rebook," or "booked online but got no confirmation" → patient coordinator/communication gap
- Response rate to reviews: if they don't respond, they're not reputation-managing actively

**Indeed job posting check:**
- Hiring "Patient Coordinator" → consultation conversion or rebooking problem
- Hiring "Medical Aesthetics Injector" with competitive pay → staff shortage, injector dependency risk
- Hiring "Social Media Manager" → marketing chaos or outsourcing need
- Multiple open positions simultaneously → operational strain

## Phase 3: The 5-Email Med Spa Sequence

### Med Spa Email Writing Rules

**Length:** Under 85 words for emails 1-3. Under 65 words for emails 4-5.

**Voice rules for med spa:**
- Write like you understand the aesthetics business, not like a healthcare vendor
- Reference their treatments by name (Botox, filler, Morpheus8) — it signals you know the market
- Speak to the business owner first, the medical professional second
- Use retention/revenue language, not clinical language
- Clean, minimal formatting — no bullet points in cold emails. Short paragraphs only.

**Subject lines that work in med spa:**
- "your rebooking rate"
- "first-time client retention"
- "what [Practice] in [City] found"
- "your Botox clients at month 4"
- "consultation conversion question"
- "noticed something in your reviews"

## Phase 4: Instagram DM Strategy (Med Spa-Specific)

Instagram is unique to this vertical. Med spa owners are highly active on Instagram and many actively DM with peers, vendors they respect, and influencers. This channel works when handled correctly.

**Rules:**
- Follow the practice account first (Day 0), engage with content genuinely (like + comment substantively) — do NOT DM cold without prior engagement
- Wait 24-48 hours after commenting before DMing
- DM should be permission-based: "Would it be okay to send you something specific to [practice name]?" — not a pitch
- If they say yes, send a short voice note or direct link to a relevant case study
- Do NOT send a PDF, brochure, or long message in a DM

**Instagram DM sequence:**
- Day 0: Follow + leave a thoughtful comment on a before/after or educational post
- Day 2: Send permission DM (see Email 1 section above)
- Day 7: If no DM response, send Email 2 via email
- Day 14: If still no response, reference your Instagram interaction in Email 4: "I commented on your post about [topic] last week — wanted to close the loop here"
