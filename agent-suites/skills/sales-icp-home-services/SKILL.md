## Vertical Context Pre-Load

**IMPORTANT:** This skill is pre-loaded with Home Services vertical knowledge. Do NOT treat the prospect as a generic B2B company. Apply all home services context below throughout every section of the ICP output.

**Vertical:** Home Services (field service businesses — HVAC, Plumbing, Roofing, Electrical, Pest Control, Landscaping, Pool Service, Cleaning, General Contracting)

**Core business model:** Revenue generated by dispatching skilled technicians to residential/commercial job sites. Thin margins (10-20% net), seasonal revenue swings, owner-operated, cash businesses. Fundamentally different from SaaS or professional services.

**Economic reality:**
- 1 truck ≈ $200K–$400K annual revenue (use as budget proxy)
- Average net margin: 10–20%
- Typical software budget: $200–$1,500/month total
- Decision cycle: Days to 2 weeks (not months)
- Decision maker: Owner/Founder in 90% of cases under 20 employees

## Purpose

You are an expert B2B sales strategist specializing in Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) development for companies selling products or services INTO the home services industry. Your job is to build a comprehensive, actionable ICP that a sales team can immediately use to identify, qualify, and prioritize home service businesses (HVAC companies, plumbers, roofers, electricians, pest control operators, landscapers, and related trades).

Every recommendation must be grounded in the operational reality of running a field service business — not generic B2B assumptions. A home service business owner is not a VP of Sales at a SaaS company. They are dispatching trucks, managing technicians, handling customer complaints, and running QuickBooks — all in the same day. Your ICP must reflect that reality.

#### Dimension 4: Pain Point Mapping

Rank and document the top home services pain points. Severity indicates how immediately the owner will spend money to fix it.

**Pain Point 1: Scheduling & Dispatch Chaos**
- **Severity:** Critical — directly stops revenue
- **Manifestation:** Whiteboard or spreadsheet, techs calling in for next job, double-bookings, customers calling "where is my tech?", owner doing dispatch from job sites on their cell phone
- **Business impact:** Each missed/late appointment = $150-400 lost revenue + potential 1-star review. 10-tech shop can lose $5K-15K/month to inefficiency.
- **Trigger events:** Adding the 4th or 5th truck, losing a customer publicly over a missed appointment, hiring an office manager for the first time
- **Current workaround:** Owner's head, whiteboard, shared Google Calendar, text message chains
- **Your solution angle:** Automated dispatch board, drag-and-drop scheduling, tech GPS, customer notification automation

**Pain Point 2: Review Volume & Rating**
- **Severity:** Critical — directly controls inbound call volume
- **Manifestation:** Sub-4.5 Google rating, fewer than 50 reviews, watching a competitor with worse work outrank them because they have 200 reviews
- **Business impact:** Each star rating point on Google is worth 5-9% more inbound calls. Gap between 3.9 and 4.7 can mean 30-40% fewer leads before the phone rings.
- **Trigger events:** Competitor newly appears in Google Maps top 3, owner receives a 1-star review they can't remove
- **Current workaround:** Verbally asking satisfied customers, handwritten card, sporadic email — no system
- **Your solution angle:** Automated post-job SMS/email review request, monitoring dashboard, response templates

**Pain Point 3: Lead Generation Cost & Quality**
- **Severity:** High — expensive and frustrating but familiar
- **Manifestation:** Paying $40-80 per shared Angi lead, closing 15-25% of those leads (shared with 3-4 competitors), feeling like they're subsidizing Angi's business
- **Business impact:** True CAC from Angi = $150-400. A $2M company can spend $120K-180K/year on Angi with mediocre ROI.
- **Trigger events:** Angi price increase, same lead sold to 5 competitors, month where Angi leads were terrible
- **Current workaround:** Paying reluctantly while complaining about it constantly
- **Your solution angle:** Owned channels (reviews → Google Maps ranking → inbound calls), LSA optimization, referral systems

**Pain Point 4: Cash Flow & Collections**
- **Severity:** High — stressful but often normalized
- **Manifestation:** Invoicing at end of week, customers paying 30-60 days late, doing the work before getting paid, chasing invoices manually
- **Business impact:** $500K revenue company with 30-day average collections = $40K+ perpetually tied up in AR. Seasonal peaks make this worse.
- **Trigger events:** Slow season cash crunch, bounced check from commercial customer, adding a truck they can barely afford
- **Current workaround:** Paper invoices, QuickBooks invoices emailed manually, occasional follow-up calls
- **Your solution angle:** On-site payment collection in field app, digital invoices with pay-now links, automated payment reminders

**Pain Point 5: Technician Recruitment & Retention**
- **Severity:** High — existential threat in tight labor market
- **Manifestation:** Good techs leaving for competitors or going independent, spending $5K+ on job boards with no results, dealing with techs who don't represent the brand well
- **Business impact:** Replacing a technician costs $8K-15K+ in recruiting, training, and productivity loss. Growth is physically constrained by tech availability.
- **Trigger events:** Lost a key tech, a tech left to start their own company, failed to staff for busy season
- **Current workaround:** Indeed, word of mouth, paying above market rates, promoting underqualified employees
- **Your solution angle:** Depends on product — if relevant, tie to employer brand, reviews on Glassdoor/Indeed, tech-facing mobile app that makes their job easier

**Pain Point 6: Seasonality & Revenue Predictability**
- **Severity:** Medium — painful but owners consider it "just the way it is"
- **Manifestation:** HVAC companies flush in July and January, broke in April and October. Roofing dead in winter. Impossible to staff consistently.
- **Business impact:** 30-50% revenue swings make planning impossible. Owners either overstaff (costly) or understaff (lose revenue when it peaks).
- **Trigger events:** Just came out of a brutal slow season, a competitor selling maintenance agreements is growing faster
- **Current workaround:** Line of credit to survive slow months, laying off techs in winter
- **Your solution angle:** Maintenance agreement/membership programs, automated service reminder campaigns, off-season marketing campaigns

#### Dimension 5: Budget Qualification

**Revenue proxies for home services (use when direct revenue data is unavailable):**

| Truck Count | Estimated Revenue | Likely Budget for Your Solution |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 trucks | $200K–$600K | $50–$200/mo — very price sensitive |
| 3-5 trucks | $600K–$1.5M | $200–$600/mo — moderate spend |
| 6-10 trucks | $1.5M–$3M | $500–$1,200/mo — will invest for ROI |
| 11-20 trucks | $3M–$6M | $1,000–$2,500/mo — budget exists, needs business case |
| 20+ trucks | $6M+ | $2,500+/mo — likely on ServiceTitan, complex decision |

**Budget signals unique to home services:**
- Active Angi/HomeAdvisor spending = confirmed marketing budget exists (often $500-3K/mo)
- Running Google Ads = confirmed digital marketing budget (often $1K-5K/mo)
- On Jobber or HCP = confirmed software budget ($50-300/mo)
- On ServiceTitan = confirmed premium software budget ($500-1,500+/mo)
- QuickBooks Online = baseline digital maturity and $50+/mo software spend

**Budget cycle for home services:**
- NOT calendar year or fiscal year like corporate B2B
- Budget decisions happen when pain is acute (reactive) or right after busy season (they have money in the bank)
- Best time to close: Right after the busy season peak (HVAC: August-September, Roofing: October-November) when cash is there and they're thinking about next year
- Worst time to try to close: Middle of peak season (they're too busy) and dead of slow season (no cash)

**Deal size sweet spot by segment:**
- Owner-operator (1-5 trucks): $99–$399/month
- Growing operator (5-15 trucks): $299–$799/month
- Established operator (15-30 trucks): $700–$1,500/month

**Payment sensitivity:**
- Annual pay-upfront is a hard sell to most home service owners (cash flow concerns)
- Monthly billing preferred
- Free trial or pilot period dramatically increases close rate (they are skeptical of salespeople)

#### Dimension 6: Channel Preferences

**How home service business owners find and evaluate solutions:**

- **Peer recommendation** is the #1 channel — if another owner they trust vouches for something, they will buy within a week. Get into Facebook groups and create advocates.
- **Google search** — they Google their problem ("how to get more reviews for my HVAC company"), not your product name
- **YouTube** — they watch tutorials and vlogs from other contractors; educational content that mentions your product works well
- **Cold call** — still works in this industry, especially early morning (7-9am). They are not on LinkedIn, they don't open cold emails. Phone is alive here.
- **Facebook/Instagram ads** — targeted by business category, location. They scroll social media. This works.
- **Trade association events** — trust is high among peers at these events

**Engagement approach:**
- Keep first touchpoint under 8 minutes of their time — they will disengage if you go longer without showing clear value
- Lead with a specific number from their world ("you have 32 reviews and a 4.1 — I can show you how companies like yours get to 4.8 with 150+ reviews in 60 days")
- Offer a free audit or quick win before asking for money (e.g., "I'll show you where your Google profile is losing calls for free")
- Reference another contractor in their trade or geography who got results (peer credibility trumps everything)
- Do not use enterprise sales tactics — no NDAs, no 30-page proposals, no legal review cycles

**Trust signals that work in home services:**
- Contractor testimonials with specific before/after numbers ("Jake's HVAC went from 3.8 to 4.7 and added 14 inbound calls/month")
- Integration with tools they already use (Jobber, HCP, ServiceTitan)
- Local presence or knowledge of their specific market
- No long-term contract (month-to-month preferred)
- Visible case studies from contractors in the same trade

### Step 4: Create the ICP Scoring Rubric

**Home Services-adjusted scoring weights:**

| Category | Max Points | Home Services Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Firmographic Fit | 20 | Reduced from 25 — trade and truck count matter more than standard firmographics |
| Technographic Fit | 15 | Same — current FSM signals are highly predictive |
| Pain Point Alignment | 25 | Increased from 20 — pain is the primary driver of home services buying decisions |
| Budget Capacity | 20 | Same — use truck count and ad spend as proxies |
| Contact Access | 10 | Same — reaching the owner directly is the key variable |
| Timing Signals | 10 | Same — seasonal timing replaces budget cycle |

**Detailed scoring guide:**

**Firmographic Fit (20 points):**
- **20 points:** In primary target trade, 5-20 trucks, 3+ years in business, residential-first, in target metro area
- **15 points:** In primary trade with one gap (e.g., 3 trucks, or mixed resi/commercial, or slightly smaller market)
- **10 points:** Adjacent trade (e.g., landscaping vs HVAC), or at edge of revenue range
- **5 points:** Secondary trade, small operator (2-3 trucks), or secondary geography
- **0 points:** Pure commercial, under 18 months old, or solo operator under $200K

**Technographic Fit (15 points):**
- **15 points:** On Jobber or HCP (confirmed software buyer, pain with current tool visible, not on ServiceTitan), active on Angi/HomeAdvisor
- **11 points:** On spreadsheet/whiteboard (high need, no competing software), or on older FSM (FieldEdge, Successware — upgrade candidate)
- **8 points:** On ServiceTitan but identified gap your product fills; or has Google Ads (confirmed marketing budget)
- **4 points:** Minimal tech stack — only QuickBooks and Gmail, low sophistication
- **0 points:** No digital presence, no known software tools, no marketing spend

**Pain Point Alignment (25 points):**
- **25 points:** Google rating under 4.5 AND fewer than 75 reviews AND active Angi spend — triple pain confirmation
- **19 points:** Two confirmed pain signals (e.g., sub-4.5 rating + no review system, OR Angi spend + no owned lead gen)
- **13 points:** One confirmed pain signal with supporting indicators
- **6 points:** Likely experiencing pain based on company profile but no direct evidence
- **0 points:** 4.8+ rating, 200+ reviews, owned lead gen, no Angi dependency — not a target for review/lead gen tools (different product fit needed)

**Budget Capacity (20 points):**
- **20 points:** 8-20 trucks, active Google Ads or Angi spend over $1K/mo, on HCP or Jobber (confirmed software spend)
- **15 points:** 5-8 trucks, some paid advertising, existing software subscription
- **10 points:** 3-5 trucks, limited advertising, QuickBooks only
- **5 points:** 2-3 trucks, no paid advertising, cash-only mindset
- **0 points:** 1 truck, no digital spend, explicitly price-sensitive / in financial distress

**Contact Access (10 points):**
- **10 points:** Owner name and cell phone on Google Business Profile or website
- **8 points:** Owner name on LinkedIn or Facebook, can find contact info
- **5 points:** Business phone only, owner name not publicly visible but findable
- **2 points:** Only contact form, no direct access to owner
- **0 points:** Franchise or corporate-owned (owner is not the economic buyer)

**Timing Signals (10 points):**
- **10 points:** Actively hiring office manager or CSR (growth signal), just came out of peak season (has cash), or posted about specific pain in last 30 days
- **8 points:** Posting jobs for technicians (growing), recent bad review they responded to (pain awareness)
- **5 points:** In shoulder season (approaching but not in peak), recent Google Business Profile update (active interest in visibility)
- **2 points:** Deep in peak season (too busy) or known slow season (no cash)
- **0 points:** Just renewed contract with competitor, just went through major life event (merger, closure, retirement announced)

**Grade Bands:**
- **A+ (90-100):** Call within 24 hours. Owner is reachable, pain is confirmed, timing is right, budget exists. This is a same-week close opportunity.
- **A (75-89):** High priority. Strong fit. Personalized outreach, lead with a specific observation about their Google presence or Angi spend.
- **B (60-74):** Good fit. Add to calling sequence. Reference local contractors in their trade for credibility.
- **C (40-59):** Marginal. Add to Facebook/email nurture. Call only if pipeline is thin.
- **D (0-39):** Do not pursue. Wrong size, wrong stage, or no budget path.

**60-Second Quick Qualification:**
1. Are they in a target trade with 3+ trucks? (Y/N)
2. Is their Google rating under 4.6 or review count under 75? (Y/N)
3. Are they spending on Angi, Thumbtack, or Google Ads? (Y/N)
4. Can you find the owner's name and phone number publicly? (Y/N)
5. Is it shoulder season or just post-peak (not dead of slow season or peak)? (Y/N)

5 Yes = A grade. 3-4 Yes = B grade. 1-2 Yes = C grade. 0 Yes = D grade.

### Step 6: Prospecting Playbook

**Where to find home service prospects:**

1. **Google Maps** — Search "[trade] [city]" in your target markets. Look at page 2-3 results and filter by those with ratings under 4.6 or fewer than 75 reviews. They need what you have.
   - Search string: `HVAC repair [city] site:google.com/maps` or simply search in Google Maps
   - Look for: rating, review count, "responds to reviews" status, website quality

2. **Angi / HomeAdvisor Pro Directory** — Lists active contractors by category and zip code. Contractors actively on Angi are confirmed buyers of lead gen services and are often frustrated.

3. **BBB (bbb.org)** — Search by business category. Accredited members tend to be established, quality-conscious operators.

4. **Indeed / ZipRecruiter** — Search "HVAC technician [city]" or "plumber [city]." Companies hiring techs are growing and have budget. The job description often reveals the FSM they use.
   - Bonus: Companies hiring "office manager" or "customer service rep" for a home service company = strong growth signal

5. **Facebook Groups** — Search for "[HVAC/Plumbing/Roofing] business owners" groups. Lurk and note who is posting about problems you solve. Direct outreach in groups (if allowed) or via Facebook Messenger.

6. **Local Chamber of Commerce directories** — Especially valuable in mid-sized markets where home service companies are prominent members.

7. **Yelp for Business** — Search by category. Filter by review count. Companies with 10-50 reviews are in the gap between "just started" and "figured it out" — prime targets.

**Outreach timing (critical for home services):**
- **Best:** Tuesday–Thursday, 7:30–9:00am (before dispatch craziness) or 6:00–7:00pm (after last job)
- **Best seasonal timing:** Shoulder seasons — HVAC in March-April and September-October, Roofing in October-November, Plumbing anytime
- **Avoid:** Monday mornings (chaos), Friday afternoons (done), Saturday, and peak season mid-day

**60-second pre-outreach checklist:**
1. ☐ Google rating under 4.6?
2. ☐ Fewer than 75 Google reviews?
3. ☐ Active Angi/HomeAdvisor profile?
4. ☐ Website last updated 2+ years ago?
5. ☐ Owner name findable on Google Business Profile or website?
6. ☐ Is it a non-peak, non-dead season in their trade?
7. ☐ Any recent bad reviews they responded to (or didn't respond to)?

5+ checkmarks = call today.

**Opening lines that work for home services:**
- "I looked up your Google profile — you've got [X] reviews and a [rating]. I help [trade] companies in [city] get that above 4.8 without bugging customers manually. Got 8 minutes?"
- "I saw you're on Angi. I work with [trade] companies to replace half that Angi spend with leads they own. Quick question: what are you spending per lead right now?"
- "I noticed you responded to a 2-star review last week. Most [trade] owners I work with say that's the one thing that keeps them up at night. Want to see how companies with your truck count handle it?"
