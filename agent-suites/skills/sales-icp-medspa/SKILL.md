## Vertical Context

VERTICAL: Medical Spas (Med Spas)
SUB-TYPES: Owner-operated med spa (MD/NP/PA-owned), multi-location med spa chain, dermatology practice with aesthetics division, plastic surgery practice with med spa component, franchise med spa (Ideal Image, LaserAway)
BUYERS:
  - Economic Buyer: Owner/Medical Director (MD, DO, NP, PA) or Spa Director/CEO
  - Technical Buyer: Practice Manager / Operations Manager / Office Manager
  - Influencer: Lead Injector, Esthetician Lead, Front Desk/Patient Coordinator
  - Blocker: Pharmaceutical manufacturer marketing programs (Allergan Brilliant Distinctions, Galderma Aspire) — practices often get "free" marketing support from toxin vendors and resist paying separately
REVENUE MODEL: Cash-pay ONLY — no insurance accepted. High-margin, elective procedures. Primary revenue drivers: neurotoxins (Botox, Dysport, Xeomin), dermal fillers (Juvederm, Restylane, Sculptra), laser treatments, body contouring, medical-grade skincare retail. Membership/VIP programs growing rapidly. Avg revenue range: $500K–$5M. Top-tier multi-location: $5M–$20M+.
PRACTICE MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE: Aesthetic Record, Zenoti, Vagaro, Jane App, Mindbody, PatientNow, Boulevard, Symplast — software is fragmented; EMR + scheduling + POS all in one is the standard expectation
TERMINOLOGY: neurotoxin, toxin units (units), syringe, filler, cannula, RF microneedling, body contouring, CoolSculpting, Morpheus8, IPL, BBL, skin resurfacing, medical director, injector, esthetician, treatment room, downtime, before/after photos, pre/post care, upsell, rebooking rate, average ticket, membership, VIP program, retention rate, RealSelf, patient coordinator, consultation conversion rate
COMPLIANCE: HIPAA, state medical board (scope of practice for NPs/PAs varies by state — always check), FDA (device clearances for lasers/devices), state cosmetology board (for esthetic services), photo release/consent, prescription requirements for toxins and fillers
KEY PAIN POINTS:
  1. Client retention and rebooking — average med spa loses 40-60% of first-time clients who never rebook
  2. Injector/staff recruitment and retention — experienced injectors are in short supply and command premium wages
  3. Marketing ROI confusion — spending on Instagram, Google Ads, and influencers with no clear attribution
  4. Consultation conversion rate — consultations that don't convert to booked treatments (industry avg 40-60%, best-in-class 75%+)
  5. Before/after photo management and consent — managing photos for social media, compliance, and RealSelf
  6. Inventory management — toxins and fillers expire, have high unit cost, and require DEA tracking
BUDGET SIGNALS: Multiple treatment rooms (4+), multiple injectors on staff, in-house laser/body contouring devices (CoolSculpting, Morpheus8, Moxi), active membership program, running paid social ads, RealSelf Pro profile, branded retail skincare line (SkinMedica, Obagi, iS Clinical)
SEASONAL: Strong: January-February (New Year's + Valentine's Day), April-May (wedding/spring season), September-October (fall refresh pre-holiday), November-December (holiday gifting). Slow: mid-summer (outdoor season, less concern about bruising/downtime), early January (post-holiday budget reset before spending returns).
DATA SOURCES: State medical board license lookup, AmSpa (American Med Spa Association) member directory, RealSelf Pro directory, Google Business Profile, Instagram, Yelp, ClassPass/Mindbody booking platform visibility

### Dimension 4: Pain Point Map (Med Spa-Specific)

**Pain 1 — Client Retention & Rebooking (CRITICAL)**
- Severity: Critical — industry standard is 40-60% of first-time clients never return. Best-in-class is 80%+ rebooking.
- How it manifests: Revenue spikes when promos run, crashes after. Constant new client acquisition cost without retention.
- Business impact: A $1M med spa with 50% retention vs. 70% retention loses $200K-$400K in annual recurring revenue
- Current workaround: Manual calls from the front desk ("Are you interested in coming in?"), generic email blasts
- Your angle: Automated rebooking sequences, anniversary messaging, treatment cycle reminders ("Your Botox is due in 3 months")

**Pain 2 — Consultation Conversion Rate (HIGH)**
- Severity: High — the industry average consultation conversion is 40-60%. Every 1% improvement = significant revenue.
- How it manifests: Consultations are free or low-cost ($50 credited toward treatment). Many clients "think about it" and never book.
- Business impact: At 100 consultations/month × 50% conversion × $500 avg treatment = $25K. At 70% = $35K. That's $10K/month difference.
- Current workaround: Paper treatment plans, manual follow-up calls, no structured sequence
- Your angle: Post-consultation follow-up automation, digital treatment plan delivery with "book now" embedded

**Pain 3 — Marketing Attribution (HIGH)**
- Severity: High — most med spas spend $5K-$20K/month on Instagram ads, Google Ads, and influencers with no clear attribution
- How it manifests: Owner knows they're "getting clients from Instagram" but can't prove ROI. Agency invoices look good, results are unclear.
- Business impact: 20-40% of marketing budget wasted on channels with no attribution
- Current workaround: Monthly reports from the agency that look impressive but don't connect to revenue
- Your angle: Source-to-revenue tracking, first-visit source attribution, LTV by acquisition channel

**Pain 4 — Injector/Staff Recruitment & Retention (HIGH)**
- Severity: High — experienced injectors are scarce and know it. They command $50-$120/hour and have leverage.
- How it manifests: Owner is personally injecting 50+ hours/week because she can't find help. Or a key injector left and took their clients.
- Business impact: Losing one injector can cost $200-$500K in annual revenue
- Current workaround: Paying premium wages, non-competes (often unenforceable), injector bonuses tied to revenue
- Your angle: Anything that makes the injector's job easier, reduces charting time, or improves client experience (which keeps injectors' books full)

**Pain 5 — Before/After Photo & Consent Management (MEDIUM-HIGH)**
- Severity: Medium-high — HIPAA compliance for photos is complex. Most practices manage photos in a mix of iPhone camera rolls, Google Drive, and EMR.
- How it manifests: Can't find before photos, posting photos without proper consent, photos not linked to patient records
- Business impact: A HIPAA violation from a photo incident can cost $10K-$50K+. More commonly, inability to use photos for marketing is a growth limiter.
- Your angle: HIPAA-compliant photo management with integrated consent and social media workflow

### Dimension 5: Budget Qualification (Med Spa-Specific)

- **Minimum viable revenue:** $600K annual revenue
- **Technology budget:** Med spas spend 3-6% of revenue on software and marketing tech ($20K-$120K/year for a $1M-$2M practice)
- **Deal size sweet spot:** $300-$2,000/month for SaaS; $5K-$25K for one-time implementations or annual contracts
- **Budget cycle:** Cash-pay business = cash flow is real-time. Decisions made based on cash position, not fiscal calendar. Spring and September are strong buying windows.
- **Budget authority:** Owner-operated: single decision-maker, often decides within 1-2 weeks. Multi-location: COO/Spa Director has budget up to $2K/month; above that, owner involved.
- **ROI language that resonates:** Revenue per treatment room per day, client rebooking rate, average ticket size, cost per new patient, consultation conversion rate, LTV per client — always connect to their cash-pay revenue metrics

**External budget signals:**
- CoolSculpting, Morpheus8, or Sciton devices visible on website → $100K+ capital investment = budget appetite
- Running Alle (Allergan) or Aspire (Galderma) loyalty program → invested in client retention ecosystem
- Active RealSelf Pro profile ($300-$1,500/month) → paying for leads = marketing budget exists
- Facebook Ad Library shows active ads → paid media budget confirmed
- Mindbody or Zenoti subscription → paying for tech already

### Dimension 6: Channel Preferences (Med Spa-Specific)

**Ranked by effectiveness for med spas:**
1. **Peer referral from another med spa owner** — "My friend in Austin uses this" is the most powerful close
2. **AmSpa MedSpa Show / aesthetics conference exhibit** — owners are there to evaluate tools
3. **Instagram DM or comment engagement** — unique to this vertical; med spa owners are highly active on IG
4. **Cold email to owner** — works if specific, short, references a real metric ("practices like yours see X% rebooking improvement")
5. **Facebook group engagement** — be genuinely helpful in Medical Spa Owners groups before pitching
6. **Google Ads retargeting** — effective because they're already searching for solutions
7. **Cold call to the practice** — lower effectiveness; owners and managers are often in treatment rooms

**Decision-making process:**
- Solo owner: Discovers → evaluates alone or with practice manager → decides in 1-3 weeks
- Partnership-owned: Partners must align — add 2-4 weeks for consensus
- Multi-location operator: COO/Director evaluates → Owner approves if over budget threshold
- Franchise/chain: Corporate procurement only — do not pursue at the local level

**Content that converts med spa buyers:**
- Before/after not of treatments — but of the business metrics. "Before: 48% rebooking rate. After: 71%."
- Peer video testimonial from a med spa owner in a similar market (not competitor market)
- Live demo showing how it looks inside Aesthetic Record or Zenoti — they need to see it in their world
- ROI calculator: "Enter your monthly consultations and current rebooking rate to see your monthly revenue gap"
- Instagram-native content — this audience trusts what looks good

## Negative ICP (Med Spa-Specific Disqualifiers)

1. **Franchise-owned (Ideal Image, LaserAway, SEV Laser)** — corporate purchasing, no local authority
2. **Practicing under Allergan or Galderma exclusive marketing agreements** — those agreements often restrict third-party vendor spend

4. **Practice collecting under $500K/year** — pre-profit, every dollar goes to payroll and toxin inventory
5. **Owner who opened the practice in the last 12 months** — still building the foundation, not ready for optimization tools
6. **Practices where the owner injector is the only injector** — no capacity to scale, every dollar goes to labor
7. **Non-compliant practices** — no HIPAA BAA, no proper medical director — won't implement compliant tools
8. **Practices in heavy discount spiral** — running Groupon, deeply discount-dependent — they can't afford retention tools and don't believe in full-price value
9. **Salon with "medical" branding but no actual medical director** — compliance risk, not a real med spa
10. **Practice actively for sale** — owner is not investing in tools, focused on exit multiple

## Buyer Personas (Med Spa-Specific)

### Persona 1: "The Injector-Turned-Entrepreneur" (Owner NP/PA/MD)
- **Title:** Owner / Medical Director, NP or PA, age 32-48
- **Day-in-the-life:** Injecting patients 9am-5pm, managing staff texts in between appointments, checking the booking system on breaks, answering vendor emails at 9pm. Became an injector for the artistry, now running a full business.
- **Goals/KPIs:** Revenue per treatment room per day, client retention rate, average ticket size, cost per new client
- **Top frustrations:** Clients come for Botox once and disappear. Staff turnover. Marketing spend with no clear ROI. Can't get off the injecting table to run the business.
- **Information diet:** Instagram obsessively, The Wealthy Injector podcast, AmSpa webinars, Medical Spa Owners Facebook group, Allergan/Galderma training events
- **Objections:** "I'm already getting marketing support from Allergan." "Does this integrate with Aesthetic Record?" "I don't have time to onboard something new." "I tried [similar tool] and my staff didn't use it."
- **Messaging:** "Your rebooking rate for Botox clients should be 80%. If it's under 60%, you're leaving $X on the table every month." "Allergan's marketing tools are for awareness — this is for retention, which is where your revenue actually lives."
- **What turns them off:** Anything that sounds like "more work for my team." Vendors who don't know what a toxin unit is. Overly corporate language. Pricing that isn't transparent.

### Persona 2: "The Med Spa Operations Director"
- **Title:** Practice Manager / Spa Director / Operations Manager, age 28-42
- **Day-in-the-life:** First and last in the door. Manages scheduling, staff, inventory (toxins and fillers are expensive and she counts every unit), client complaints, and vendor relationships. Reports to the owner.
- **Goals/KPIs:** Schedule utilization rate, rebooking rate, new vs. returning client split, staff no-call-no-shows, daily revenue vs. goal
- **Objections:** "The owner needs to approve this." "Does it integrate with Zenoti/Aesthetic Record?" "We just changed our booking system and the team is not ready for more changes."
- **Messaging:** "You get one dashboard instead of three tabs. Your front desk will thank you." "Setup takes one afternoon — we handle migration, you just walk in the next day."
- **What turns them off:** Vendors who go over her head to the owner. Tools that require IT expertise she doesn't have. Anything that adds steps to the front desk booking flow.

### Persona 3: "The Multi-Location Med Spa CEO"
- **Title:** CEO / Founder / COO, owns 3-10 locations, age 38-55
- **Day-in-the-life:** No longer injecting (or rarely). Lives in a dashboard. Focused on EBITDA, location-level performance, injector headcount, and marketing ROI across locations.
- **Goals/KPIs:** Revenue per location per month, EBITDA margin, client acquisition cost by location, retention rate by injector
- **Objections:** "We need multi-location reporting." "Show me the enterprise pricing." "We already have a tech stack — what does this replace?" "We need a data security review."
- **Messaging:** "Here's the dashboard view across 5 locations — production by injector, retention by location, revenue by service line." "We have 3 other med spa groups on multi-location plans. Here's what they see."
- **What turns them off:** Single-location pitch. No enterprise pricing. Lack of multi-location reporting. Inability to discuss SOC 2 or HIPAA at a technical level.

## Prospecting Playbook (Med Spa-Specific)

**Where to find med spas:**
- **Google Maps:** Search "med spa [city]" or "medical aesthetics [city]" — stars, review count, and photos reveal positioning instantly
- **RealSelf Pro directory** (realself.com/find): Lists licensed providers, specialties, location — self-curated by practices investing in lead gen
- **AmSpa member directory** (americanmedspa.org): Members are invested in the industry and professional development
- **Instagram:** Search hashtags #medspa[city], #botox[city], #aestheticmedicine — find active practices by geography
- **State medical board license lookup:** Find owner NP/PA/MD name by searching for the practice address
- **Facebook groups:** Medical Spa Owners Group, Aesthetic Business Owners — lurk to understand pain, engage to build trust

**Search strings:**
```
Instagram: #medspa[city] OR #botox[city] OR "medical spa [city]"
Google: "med spa" "[city]" reviews "never came back" OR "hard to rebook"
LinkedIn: "medical spa" AND "owner" AND "[city]"
Indeed: "medical spa" AND "patient coordinator" AND "[city]" (hiring = pain signal)
```

**Timing tactics:**
- Best outreach window: January 5-20 (post-holiday, pre-Valentine's Day prep), September 1-15 (pre-holiday season planning)
- Second best: Post-AmSpa MedSpa Show (March/April) — owners just returned with new vendor appetite
- Avoid: Thanksgiving week, December 15-31 (busiest treatment season), mid-summer
- Best outreach method: Email + Instagram DM combo — this audience lives on Instagram
- Best time: 7-8am (before first patient) or 7-8pm (after last patient)

**First 3 disqualification checks:**
1. Does the website say Ideal Image, LaserAway, or SEV Laser? (Franchise — different motion)
2. Is it listed as a "day spa" or "beauty spa" with no medical director listed? (Not a true med spa)
3. Does the Google listing show "Temporarily closed" or review activity stopped 6+ months ago? (Winding down)

## Competitive Context (Med Spa-Specific)

**Category competitors:**
- **Patient engagement/retention:** Boulevard, Zenoti (built-in), Aesthetic Record (built-in), PatientFi
- **Loyalty programs:** Alle (Allergan), Aspire (Galderma) — these are "free" from manufacturers and compete directly with paid retention tools
- **Marketing platforms:** PatientPop (Spotlight), Podium, Birdeye, Weave (cross-vertical)
- **Before/after photo management:** TouchMD, Canfield (VISIA), built into Aesthetic Record
- **Lead gen:** RealSelf Pro, Google Ads agencies, Instagram ad agencies

**Key displacement:** Alle and Aspire (manufacturer loyalty programs) are the most common "we already have that" response. Counter: "Alle and Aspire track which brand of toxin they use — they're loyalty to Allergan/Galderma, not to your practice. This tracks loyalty to YOUR practice regardless of which toxin you use."

**Market trend:** The med spa industry is growing at 14% CAGR, but new practices are opening at an even faster rate — competition is intensifying. Established practices increasingly need retention and differentiation tools to maintain market share against newer, flashier competitors. Frame your solution as "compete on experience, not just results."
