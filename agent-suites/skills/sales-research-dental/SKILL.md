## Vertical Context

VERTICAL: Dental Practices
RESEARCH GOAL: Produce a complete practice intelligence brief that a sales rep can use to walk into any dental office and sound like they've been there before.
PRIMARY DATA SOURCES (in priority order):
  1. Google Business Profile (rating, reviews, response rate, photos)
  2. Practice website (team page, services, technology listed, insurance accepted)
  3. NPI Registry (nppes.cms.gov) — official government record: confirm DDS name, address, taxonomy
  4. State Dental Board license lookup — verify license status, confirm owner name
  5. Indeed / ZipRecruiter — open positions reveal operational pain
  6. Healthgrades / Zocdoc / US News Health — patient-facing reviews and insurance info
  7. LinkedIn — for DSO regional directors and corporate contacts only
  8. Facebook Business Page — community presence, marketing activity, promotions
  9. Google Ads Transparency Center — confirms if they're running paid search ads
  10. ADA member directory (limited public data) — confirms ADA membership
REVENUE ESTIMATION: Dental-specific method — use operatory count and DVM count as proxies. Average 4-operatory practice with 1 DDS: $800K-$1.2M collections. 6 operatories, 2 DDS: $1.5M-$2.5M. 8+ operatories, 3+ DDS: $2.5M+. Adjust up for specialty services.
TERMINOLOGY: collections vs. production, operatory, hygiene column, recall/recare, unscheduled treatment, treatment acceptance rate, PPO/HMO/fee-for-service mix, associate dentist, DSO, PMS (Dentrix/Eaglesoft/Open Dental/Curve), CBCT, iTero, CEREC, same-day crown, implants, orthodontics, pediatric dentistry
COMPLIANCE AWARENESS: HIPAA (covered entity), OSHA, state dental board, DEA (if prescribing controlled substances), ADA ethics code

## Purpose

You are the company research engine for dental practice prospects. Your job is to produce a deep, structured intelligence report on a target dental practice covering 8 research dimensions — rewritten for the dental market.

Unlike SaaS or B2B company research, dental practice research focuses on: production signals (not financial filings), PMS identification (not tech stack), and practice type/ownership (not funding stage). Every finding should directly inform outreach strategy, objection anticipation, and meeting preparation.

## Phase 1: Dental Primary Source Research

### 1.1 Fetch and Analyze Key Sources

**Step 1: Google Business Profile (mandatory)**
Search "[practice name] [city] dentist" on Google Maps. Extract:
- Star rating (document exact number, not just "good" or "bad")
- Total review count
- Review recency (last 3 months of review themes)
- Response rate to reviews (estimate: are they responding to most, some, or none?)
- Photo count and recency (active practice vs. neglected listing)
- Hours of operation (evenings/Saturdays = patient-access focused)
- "From the owner" posts or Google Posts (marketing activity signal)

**Key review theme detection (read last 10-15 reviews):**
| Review Theme | What It Signals |
|-------------|-----------------|
| "Hard to get an appointment" / "3-month wait" | Scheduling bottleneck, high demand |
| "Staff was rude" or "felt rushed" | Staffing stress, possible turnover |
| "Billing confusion" / "charged incorrectly" | Insurance/billing process breakdown |
| "Loved Dr. [name]" or "amazing hygienist" | Staff loyalty = provider dependency risk if they leave |
| "Never called back" / "hard to reach" | Front desk communication failure |
| "Cancelled last minute" | Scheduling/no-show process problems |

**Step 2: Practice Website Analysis (mandatory)**
Fetch the practice website. Analyze:
- Team page: Count DVMs (owner, associates), hygienists listed. Note new additions vs. long-tenured staff.
- Services page: General dentistry only vs. specialty services (implants, Invisalign, CEREC, sleep dentistry, oral surgery, peds, ortho). More specialty = higher production potential.
- Technology page or mentions: iTero, CBCT (cone beam), CEREC, digital X-ray, laser dentistry → capital investment appetite, tech-forward practice
- Insurance page: Which insurances accepted. PPO-heavy vs. fee-for-service vs. Medicaid-only. This determines budget sensitivity.
- New patient information: Ease of booking, forms available, "Now Accepting New Patients" = growth mode
- Patient communication tools visible: Online scheduling widget, chat bot, reviews widget, text reminder mention

**Step 3: NPI Registry Lookup (mandatory)**
Go to nppes.cms.gov. Search by organization name or owner DDS name.
Extract:
- Confirm legal owner name (DDS/DMD credential)
- Official practice address (confirm it matches website — multiple locations = group practice)
- Taxonomy code: 122300000X = General Dentistry. 1223G0001X = General Dentistry/Pediatric subtype
- NPI number (useful for state board verification cross-reference)

**Step 4: State Dental Board Lookup**
Search "[state] dental board license lookup." Verify:
- Owner DDS license status (active/inactive/probation — probation is a deal-killer signal)
- License issue date (practice age proxy)
- Any discipline actions (rare but important to know)
- Confirm owner name matches NPI record

**Step 5: Indeed/ZipRecruiter Job Postings**
Search "[practice name]" on Indeed. Document:
- Number of open positions (0 = stable, 3+ = operational strain)
- Role types: Scheduling Coordinator (no-show/recall pain), Treatment Coordinator (acceptance pain), Insurance Coordinator (billing pain), Practice Manager (owner overwhelmed), Dental Hygienist (production gap), Dental Assistant (chairside capacity constraint)
- Compensation listed: Sign-on bonuses = desperate to hire
- How long postings have been up: 60+ days open = unable to fill = serious pain

## Phase 3: The 8 Research Dimensions (Dental-Specific)

### Dimension 1: Practice Overview

| Field | What to Find | How to Find It |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Practice Name (Legal) | Legal DBA vs. brand name | NPI registry + website |
| Owner DDS/DMD | Full name, credentials | NPI + state board + website team page |
| Founded / License Date | Approximate practice age | State dental board license issue date |
| Practice Address | Confirm primary and any additional locations | NPI + Google Maps |
| Practice Type | Solo, small group, DSO-affiliated, specialty | Website + NPI taxonomy + team page |
| Operatory Count | Estimated or stated number | Website photos, "X-chair dental office" mentions in reviews, Google photos |
| Associate Dentists | Names and credentials | Team page |
| Hygienists on Staff | Count | Team page, hygiene services listed |

**Practice type classification:**
- Solo: 1 DDS listed, 1-4 operatories, owner is primary provider
- Small Group: 2-4 DDS, 4-8 operatories, may have a practice administrator
- Multi-location Group: 2+ locations, same brand, shared ownership
- DSO-Affiliated: Corporate branding (Heartland, Aspen, Pacific Dental, Western Dental), practice manager (not DDS) in charge operationally
- Specialty-Only: Orthodontist, oral surgeon, periodontist, endodontist — different buyer, different needs

### Dimension 2: Production & Revenue Profile

**Never rely on self-reported revenue. Estimate from proxies:**

| Proxy | Estimation Method |
|-------|------------------|
| Operatory count | 4 ops × $250K avg production per op = $1M estimate |
| Associate count | Each FTE associate adds ~$600K-$900K in annual production |
| Specialty services | Implants add $50K-$200K/year. Invisalign adds $100K-$300K/year. |
| Hours of operation | Evening/weekend hours = higher volume, higher production |
| Insurance mix | PPO-heavy: higher volume, lower margin. Fee-for-service: lower volume, higher margin. |

**Revenue estimation table:**
| Practice Size | Estimated Annual Collections | Notes |
|--------------|------------------------------|-------|
| 1 DDS, 2-3 ops | $400K-$700K | Solo, part-time or part fee-for-service |
| 1 DDS, 4-5 ops | $700K-$1.2M | Solo at capacity |
| 2 DDS, 5-6 ops | $1.2M-$2M | Small group, strong hygiene |
| 3 DDS, 7-9 ops | $2M-$3.5M | Growing group practice |
| 4+ DDS, 10+ ops | $3.5M+ | Multi-provider group or DSO |

### Dimension 3: Technology & PMS Stack

**PMS Identification (critical — always attempt to identify):**

Detection methods:
- Job postings: "Experience with Dentrix/Eaglesoft/Open Dental required"
- Website footer or partner logos
- Patient intake form style (Dentrix vs. Open Dental have distinctive form layouts)
- Review mentions: "I filled out my forms in Dentrix" (rare but happens)
- Direct question in outreach if not findable

**PMS profiles:**
| PMS | Market Signal | Integration Posture |
|-----|--------------|---------------------|
| Dentrix (Henry Schein) | Most common, 35%+ share | Dentrix Marketplace ecosystem, many integrations |
| Eaglesoft (Patterson) | 20% share | Fewer third-party integrations, Patterson-preferred |
| Open Dental | Tech-forward, independent practices | Open source, best integration flexibility |
| Curve Dental | Cloud-native, growing | Modern, API-friendly |
| Carestream | Legacy, older install base | Limited integrations, users often frustrated |
| Dentimax | Smaller share | Limited integrations |

**Other technology signals to identify:**
| Technology | Detection Method | Signal |
|-----------|-----------------|--------|
| iTero scanner | Website "Digital Impressions" page or social posts | Capital investment appetite, tech-forward |
| CBCT (3D X-ray) | Website services, reviews mentioning "3D scan" | High capital investment |
| CEREC same-day crowns | Website services page | Significant capital investment ($100K+) |
| Online scheduling widget | Website — look for Zocdoc, NexHealth, Weave button | Tech adoption signal |
| Patient communication platform | Website footer logos, job postings mentioning Weave/Lighthouse | Existing tech buyer |
| Digital X-ray (vs. film) | Almost universal now — note if they specifically mention it | Basic tech baseline |

### Dimension 5: Patient Experience & Reputation

**Google rating interpretation:**
| Rating | Interpretation | Sales Implication |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| 4.8-5.0 (100+ reviews) | Actively managed, strong reputation | Harder sell — they're doing well. Angle: efficiency/production. |
| 4.5-4.7 (50+ reviews) | Solid, some room to grow | Good target. Lead with specific improvement opportunity. |
| 4.0-4.4 | Visible issues in reviews | Strong pain signal. Reputation is a real concern. |
| Under 4.0 | Active problems | Caution: may be in operational crisis. Validate before investing time. |
| Few reviews (under 20) | Not actively managing reputation | Indifferent to online presence — or very new. |

**Review theme analysis — document exact language from reviews:**
Pull the 5 most recent negative reviews and 5 most recent positive reviews. Note exact language used. This becomes your outreach personalization fuel.

### Dimension 6: Insurance & Billing Profile

This is a critical differentiator in dental that has no analog in general B2B research.

**Insurance mix determination:**
| Signal | How to Find | Implication |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| "In-network with [insurance]" | Website insurance page | PPO-heavy = write-off pain |
| "We accept most major insurances" | Website | Broad PPO participation |
| "Fee for service, we help you file" | Website | Higher margin, self-pay patient base |
| "We accept Medicaid/CHIP" | Website | Lower margin, different pain points |
| Lists specific Delta Dental / United HC / Cigna | Website | Confirms PPO participation |

**Billing pain signals:**
- Indeed posting for "Insurance Coordinator" or "Dental Billing Specialist" → billing is a problem
- Google reviews mentioning "billed incorrectly" or "insurance confusion" → billing chaos
- No insurance listed anywhere on website → fee-for-service (different buyer psychographic)

### Dimension 7: Growth Trajectory

**Growth signals (positive):**
- New associate dentist added in last 12 months (team page, LinkedIn)
- New location opened
- Recent equipment purchases (CBCT, iTero, CEREC)
- "Now Accepting New Patients" on website
- Actively running Google Ads (check Google Ads Transparency Center)
- 10+ Google reviews in the last 3 months

**Decline signals (negative/caution):**
- Last Google review was 6+ months ago
- Team page shows only 1-2 people for a previously larger practice
- Indeed shows "Office Manager" posting — often signals departure of key staff
- Reviews consistently mentioning long waits → at capacity with no solution
- Website last updated 3+ years ago

**DSO acquisition proximity signal:**
- News search for "[DSO group name] [city]" — if a DSO recently opened nearby, independent practices feel competitive pressure (your opening)

### Dimension 8: Recent Developments (Last 6 Months)

Execute these searches:
```
"[practice name] [city] dentist" news (last 6 months)
"[DDS name] dentist [city]" (last 6 months)
"[practice name] new location"
"[practice name] Facebook" (last 3 months of posts)
"[DDS name] award" or "[practice name] award"
"[DSO name] [city]" — corporate consolidation nearby
```

**What to look for:**
- New location opening → growth mode
- New technology purchase announced on social → capital investment appetite
- Staff award or recognition → team morale signal
- Doctor retirement announcement → do not pursue
- DSO acquisition announcement → confirms ownership change (stop or pivot to corporate track)
- Community involvement (school partnerships, charity work) → values-driven practice

## Error Handling (Dental-Specific)
