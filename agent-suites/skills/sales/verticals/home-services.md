## Vertical Overview

Home services companies are field-service businesses where revenue is generated by sending skilled technicians to residential or commercial job sites. The business model is fundamentally different from SaaS or professional services: revenue is tied to truck rolls, labor hours, and job close rates — not subscription seats or retainers. Margins are thin (10-20% net), seasonality is real, and the owner is almost always the economic buyer.

**Scope of vertical:**
- HVAC (installation, repair, maintenance agreements)
- Plumbing (residential and light commercial)
- Roofing (residential replacement and repair)
- Electrical (residential service and light commercial)
- Pest Control (residential recurring)
- Landscaping / Lawn Care (residential recurring)
- Pool Service (residential recurring)
- House Cleaning / Maid Services (recurring)
- Garage Door, Appliance Repair, Handyman, General Contracting

## Buyer Map

| Role | Title | Decision Power | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic Buyer | Owner / President / Founder | Final yes/no | Often the same person dispatching calls. Skeptical of salespeople. Responds to ROI. |
| Technical Evaluator | Office Manager / CSR Manager | Influences heavily | Uses the software daily. Primary champion. If she hates it, it dies. |
| Operator (larger companies) | Operations Manager / GM | Influences | Manages techs, reviews, and dispatch. Cares about field efficiency. |
| Financial Gatekeeper | Owner's spouse / bookkeeper | Blocks | Common in family businesses. Don't ignore them. |

**Key insight:** In businesses under 10 employees, the owner IS the office manager and the dispatcher. One person, many hats. Keep your pitch short and ROI-focused — they do not have time for 45-minute demos.

## Pain Point Library

### Pain #1 — Scheduling & Dispatch Chaos (CRITICAL)
**Symptoms:** Whiteboard or spreadsheet scheduling, technicians calling in for their next job, double-bookings, missed appointments, customers calling to ask "where is the tech?"
**Business impact:** Each no-show or late arrival costs $150-400 in lost revenue and drives 1-star reviews. A shop running 10 techs can lose $5K-15K/month to scheduling inefficiency.
**Trigger:** "We can't keep up with the calls anymore" or hiring a 5th+ technician.
**Your angle:** Automated scheduling, real-time dispatch board, tech GPS tracking, customer notification automation.

### Pain #2 — Lead Gen Cost & Quality (HIGH)
**Symptoms:** Paying Angi/HomeAdvisor $30-80/shared lead, closing 10-20% of those leads, feeling like they're funding Angi's business not their own.
**Business impact:** True cost per acquired customer is $150-400 from lead marketplaces. A company doing $1M revenue can spend $80K-150K/year on Angi alone.
**Trigger:** Angi price increases, increasing competition from other contractors bidding same leads.
**Your angle:** Review generation to drive Google organic/LSA, reputation management to improve close rate on existing leads.

### Pain #3 — Review Management (HIGH)
**Symptoms:** Getting 3-star reviews from unreasonable customers that tank their Google rating, not asking for reviews systematically, watching a competitor with a 4.8 beat them despite inferior work.
**Business impact:** Each star rating point on Google is worth approximately 5-9% more calls in local search. A company at 3.9 vs 4.7 is losing 30-40% of their inbound leads before the phone rings.
**Trigger:** A competitor outranks them on Google Maps, or they got a recent bad review they can't shake.
**Your angle:** Automated post-job review requests, review monitoring, response templates.

### Pain #4 — Cash Flow & Collections (HIGH)
**Symptoms:** Invoicing on paper or at end of week, customers slow to pay, doing work before collecting payment, inconsistent follow-up on unpaid invoices.
**Business impact:** Net 30-45 day receivables on $500K revenue = $40K-60K tied up in receivables at any given time. Cash flow stress is the #1 reason home service companies fail to grow.
**Trigger:** Slow season cash crunch, adding a truck they can't afford because they're waiting on collections.
**Your angle:** On-site payment collection, digital invoicing, automated payment follow-up.

### Pain #5 — Technician Retention & Performance (MEDIUM-HIGH)
**Symptoms:** Good techs leaving for competitors, techs who don't upsell, techs who represent the brand poorly.
**Business impact:** Replacing a technician costs $8,000-15,000 in recruiting, onboarding, and productivity loss. Techs who don't upsell leave 20-40% revenue on the table per visit.
**Trigger:** Lost a key tech, growing fast and can't find good people.
**Your angle:** Performance tracking per tech, digital checklists, upsell prompts in the field app.

### Pain #6 — Seasonality & Revenue Predictability (MEDIUM)
**Symptoms:** HVAC companies feast in summer/winter, starve in shoulder months. Roofing is dead in winter. Revenue wildly unpredictable.
**Business impact:** 30-50% revenue swings season to season make it impossible to staff appropriately or plan investments.
**Trigger:** Just survived another rough offseason, or just got crushed by a competitor who sold maintenance agreements.
**Your angle:** Maintenance agreement/membership programs, automated service reminder campaigns, recurring revenue products.

## Discovery Questions (adapted BANT/MEDDIC for home services)

### Budget Discovery
- "How many trucks are you running right now?" *(Revenue proxy: 1 truck ≈ $200K-400K/year)*
- "Are you on any software right now for scheduling — Jobber, Housecall Pro, anything like that?" *(Tech spend signal)*
- "Are you running any paid ads — Google, Angi, anything?" *(Marketing budget signal)*
- "What are you spending on Angi/HomeAdvisor right now per month?" *(Budget unlock question — they almost always hate it)*

### Authority Discovery
- "Is it just you making this call, or do you have a partner or office manager who'd want to weigh in?" *(One question does the whole authority map)*
- "Who handles the scheduling day-to-day — is that you or someone else?" *(Identifies the champion/operator)*

### Need Discovery
- "What does your scheduling look like today — are you running a board, spreadsheet, something else?" *(Opens dispatch pain)*
- "How are you getting most of your new customers right now?" *(Lead gen pain)*
- "What's your Google rating sitting at? How many reviews do you have?" *(Opens review pain — owners know this number cold)*
- "Do your techs collect payment on-site or do you invoice after?" *(Opens cash flow pain)*

### Timeline Discovery
- "Are you heading into your busy season or coming out of it?" *(Seasonal timing is everything)*
- "What's on your plate right now — are you in the middle of hiring or anything like that?" *(Urgency check)*
- "When does your current [Jobber/Angi/etc.] contract renew?" *(Contract timing)*

## Objection Library (Home Services Specific)

### "I don't have time for this"
**What it really means:** They are in reactive mode. The phone is ringing and they're dispatching. This is the most common home services objection and it's legitimate.
**Response:** "I hear you — this is literally the worst time to talk to me, and I respect that. I only need 8 minutes to show you one thing. If it doesn't save your office manager 2 hours a week, hang up and never call me back. When's a slow morning for you — Tuesday before 9?"

### "We already use [Jobber/Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan]"
**What it really means:** They have an FSM but it may not solve the specific problem you're addressing (reviews, lead gen, marketing, etc.). OR they're locked in and need to be nurtured to contract renewal.
**Response:** "Perfect — that actually helps me. [Jobber/HCP] is great for dispatch. What I do is different — I work on the front end, specifically how many calls come IN before dispatch even matters. Can I show you what I mean in 5 minutes?"

### "We get all our work from referrals — we don't need marketing"
**What it really means:** They're proud of their reputation (good!) but are dangerously dependent on a channel they don't control.
**Response:** "That's actually the best sign — it means you do great work. The problem with referrals is you can't turn them up when you need more trucks rolling. What happens in your slow season when referrals dry up? I work with companies exactly like yours to turn that great reputation into a Google presence that works for you 24/7."

### "We tried [software/marketing] before and got burned"
**What it really means:** Someone sold them something that overpromised. They're risk-averse and skeptical of salespeople (with good reason).
**Response:** "I don't blame you at all — this industry has a lot of people selling shiny things that don't work in the field. Can I ask what specifically went wrong? Because if it's the same thing I do, I'll tell you right now and save us both time."

### "I can't afford it right now"
**What it really means:** Cash flow is tight (seasonal), or they don't see the ROI clearly enough yet.
**Response:** "Fair enough. Let me ask you this — what are you spending on Angi right now per month?" *(They say $800, $1,500, $3,000.)* "What I'm going to show you is how to replace half that spend with leads that don't cost per click and close at twice the rate. If the numbers don't work, I'll tell you."

### "My nephew handles the website / my buddy does the marketing"
**What it really means:** Personal relationship is protecting an incumbent. This is common in family businesses.
**Response:** "That's great — and I'm not trying to step on that relationship. Most of the owners I work with still have their nephew handle the website. What I do is specifically the review automation and Google ranking side — it's usually stuff the website guy doesn't touch. Would it make sense to show your nephew what I do so he can tell you if it overlaps?"

## ICP Scoring Adjustments for Home Services

Standard B2B BANT weights do not map cleanly to home services. Use these adjusted weights:

| BANT Dimension | Standard Weight | Home Services Weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | 25% | 20% | Harder to assess publicly; use truck count + tool spend as proxy |
| Authority | 25% | 30% | Owner almost always decides; if you can reach the owner, authority is high |
| Need | 25% | 30% | Need signals are obvious and public (reviews, ad spend, web presence) |
| Timeline | 25% | 20% | Seasonality governs timing more than internal buying cycles |

**Bonus scoring signals unique to home services:**
- Google rating below 4.5 = +10 need points
- Active Angi/HomeAdvisor profile = +8 need points (known pain)
- Fewer than 50 Google reviews = +8 need points
- Job posting for office manager or CSR = +10 timeline points (growth signal)
- 3+ trucks but still on spreadsheet/whiteboard = +15 need points

## Prospecting Playbook (Home Services)

### Where to Find Them
- **Google Maps** — Search "[trade] [city]" and look at the 2nd-3rd page results (below the well-optimized shops = opportunity)
- **Angi / HomeAdvisor Pro Listings** — Lists active contractors with profile data
- **BBB (Better Business Bureau)** — Accredited businesses in home services categories
- **Yelp for Business** — Search by category, filter by review count
- **Indeed / ZipRecruiter** — Search "[HVAC technician / plumber / roofer] [city]" to find companies actively hiring
- **Local Chamber of Commerce directories**
- **Nextdoor business listings**
- **Facebook local business groups**

### Qualification Quick Check (60 seconds)
1. Google rating under 4.6? → Need signal ✓
2. Fewer than 75 Google reviews? → Need signal ✓
3. Active on Angi/HomeAdvisor? → Lead gen pain ✓
4. Website looks outdated or has no clear CTA? → Opportunity ✓
5. Can you find the owner's name + phone from Google Business Profile? → Authority access ✓

3+ yes answers = qualified enough to call.

### Best Times to Reach Home Service Owners
- **Tuesday–Thursday, 7:30–9:00am** before dispatch craziness starts
- **Tuesday–Thursday, 6:00–7:00pm** after the last job wraps
- **Avoid:** Monday mornings (chaos), Friday afternoons (done for the week), Saturday (working)
- **Seasonal timing:** Approach HVAC in shoulder seasons (March-April, September-October) when they're not buried in installs

## Red Flags / Disqualification

| Signal | Why it Disqualifies |
|---|---|
| Under $300K revenue / 1-2 person operation | No budget, owner too busy, decision cycle too long for ROI |
| Pure commercial / B2B only | Different buying process, longer sales cycles, relationship-dependent |
| Already on ServiceTitan (if selling SMB tools) | Overbuilt and committed; unlikely to switch |
| Active family dispute or business partnership conflict | Buying decisions stalled indefinitely |
| In market decline (e.g., yellow pages only, no digital presence, 15+ year old website) | Mindset not ready; will not act on recommendations |
| Owner is near retirement with no succession plan | Won't invest in growth tools |

## Messaging That Resonates

**Opening lines that work:**
- "I looked up your Google profile — you've got [X] reviews and a [rating]. I help [trade] companies in [city] get that number up to 4.8+ without bugging your customers for reviews."
- "I saw you're running ads on Angi. I work with [trade] companies to replace Angi spend with leads they actually own. Got 8 minutes?"
- "I noticed you've got [# trucks] trucks on the road. The guys I work with at that size usually tell me scheduling and reviews are the two things keeping them up at night. Sound familiar?"

**What turns them off:**
- Long demos with no clear ROI number
- Industry jargon (say "more 5-star reviews" not "reputation management platform")
- Asking for 45-minute discovery calls before showing any value
- Talking about features before talking about the problem they care about most

**What wins them over:**
- Specific numbers ("companies like yours average 23 more reviews per month")
- Short proof ("Here's a plumber in [their city] with your same truck count — here's their before/after")
- Respecting their time ("I'll keep this to 8 minutes")
- Knowing their world ("I know you're slammed in summer — let's talk in April")
