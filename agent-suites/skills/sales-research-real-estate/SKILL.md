# Company Research & Firmographic Analysis — Real Estate & Brokerage
# Vertical: Residential Real Estate / Brokerage
# Base skill: sales-research | Patched for: real estate agents, agent teams, independent brokerages

## Vertical Context

VERTICAL: Real Estate & Brokerage
PROSPECT TYPES: Solo agent, agent team (2–20 members), independent brokerage, franchise brokerage, property management company, commercial brokerage
CORPORATE GATE: In real estate, most agents are independent contractors regardless of their brokerage brand (RE/MAX, Keller Williams, Compass, eXp, Sotheby's). The agent or team is the business unit. STOP only if the prospect is a corporate-owned office manager at a company-operated branch with no local budget authority (rare). Franchise offices are still independent businesses — do NOT treat them as corporate accounts.
WHO YOU ARE SELLING TO: Real estate agents and teams are bought to by: CRM/lead gen platforms (BoomTown, Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, Real Geeks, Chime, Sierra Interactive), mortgage companies (referral relationship / co-marketing), title companies, home warranty companies, digital marketing agencies, coaching programs (Tom Ferry, Mike Ferry, Buffini, Craig Proctor, KW MAPS), transaction management (Dotloop, Skyslope), photography/videography, direct mail vendors, virtual assistant services, lead gen portals (Zillow Premier Agent, Realtor.com)
REVENUE TERMINOLOGY: GCI (Gross Commission Income), transaction count, average sales price, under contract, pending, sold, close rate, listing-to-sold ratio, days on market, units, volume, buyer-side, listing-side, referral fee, pending pipeline, production, cap (KW cap system), splits
REVENUE ESTIMATION: GCI ≈ Transaction count × Average sale price × 2.5% commission rate. Solo agent: 10–25 transactions/year = ~$60K–$350K GCI. Productive agent: 25–50 transactions = $350K–$700K GCI. Team: 50–200 transactions = $700K–$3M GCI. Top team/brokerage: 200+ transactions.
TECH STACK CATEGORIES: CRM (BoomTown, Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, Chime, LionDesk, Real Geeks, Wise Agent), IDX Website (Real Geeks, Placester, iHomeFinder, Dakno, Agent Image, Luxury Presence), Transaction Management (Dotloop, Skyslope, DocuSign Rooms, Glide), Marketing Automation (kvCORE integrated, MailChimp, BombBomb video email), Listing Marketing (Listing Booster, HomeJab, Matterport), Showing Software (ShowingTime, Calendly), Dialer (Mojo, RedX, Vulcan7)
SEASONAL CAUTION: Spring/Summer (March–August) = peak transaction season — agents are overwhelmed with closings, difficult to reach, low receptivity to new tools. Fall/Winter (September–January) = business planning season — agents are most receptive to new tools and thinking about next year. Best prospecting window: October–December (post-summer recovery, pre-holiday). January is KW Family Reunion (major conference) — avoid that week.

You are the company research engine for real estate agent, team, and brokerage prospects. ### 1.2 Primary Data Sources for Real Estate Research

**Priority order — execute in sequence:**

**1. Zillow Agent Profile (mandatory first stop)**
Search zillow.com/agent-profile/[agent-name] or zillow.com/premier-agent/
- Recent sales: count all sold listings in last 12 months → transaction volume signal
- Average sale price range: median of sold listings → market segment signal
- Zillow Premier Agent badge present? → paid portal spend signal (~$500–$5,000+/month)
- Recent reviews: count, recency, tone (buyer vs. seller reviews signal which side they work)
- Star rating and review velocity (reviews/month): declining velocity = losing market share
- Service areas / zip codes listed → geographic farm area

**2. Realtor.com Agent Profile**
Search realtor.com/find-realtors/
- Cross-reference transaction data vs. Zillow
- Reviews from different client base
- Active listings count → current pipeline signal

**3. State Real Estate Commission License Lookup (mandatory)**
Every state has a searchable public license database:
- License type: Salesperson, Broker, Broker-Associate — Broker = higher authority/budget
- License status: Active / Inactive / Suspended — inactive = do not contact
- License date: Years in business = longevity signal (under 2 years = high churn risk)
- Brokerage affiliation: which brokerage they hang their license at
- Disciplinary history: any complaints or license actions = risk signal
- Search format: "[State] real estate license lookup" → AREC, TREC, KREC, DRE, etc.

**4. Team/Practice Website**
- Team size and composition (buyer agents, listing specialists, ISA, operations)
- CRM or tech stack visible (often in footer or job postings: "experience with kvCORE required")
- Lead gen strategy visible: IDX search tool, home valuation widget, buyer/seller landing pages
- Recent sold listings showcase → production quality and price range
- Google Ads or Meta Pixel detected in source → paid acquisition active
- Blog/market report publishing → content marketing sophistication

**5. Facebook Business Page and Facebook Ad Library**
- Facebook Ad Library: facebook.com/ads/library → search team name
  - Active lead gen ads ("Find out what your home is worth," "Search homes in [city]")
  - Active buyer ads ("Homes for sale in [neighborhood]")
  - Ad frequency: 1-3 active ads = testing; 5+ active = committed marketing spend
- Facebook Business page:
  - Follower count
  - Post frequency and recency
  - Review count and rating
  - Community engagement

**6. Instagram**
- Follower count and engagement rate
- Content type: listing posts vs. market updates vs. personal brand content
- Reel usage: video content = tech-forward agent
- Story activity: daily Stories = active engagement
- Bio: booking link, buyer/seller CTA?

**7. YouTube**
- Channel present? Agent or team with YouTube = serious content marketing investment
- Video types: home tours, neighborhood guides, market update videos = highly motivated marketer
- Subscriber count and view count → audience reach signal

**8. Indeed / ZipRecruiter Job Postings**
- Hiring "Inside Sales Agent (ISA)" → team is growing, lead gen volume is there, need conversion help
- Hiring "Transaction Coordinator" → high volume closings, scaling operations
- Hiring "Buyer's Agent" → team expansion, leader is generating more leads than they can work
- Hiring "Marketing Coordinator" or "Social Media Manager" → marketing spend is increasing
- Posting "Real Estate Agent — join our team" → brokerage recruiting, focused on growth

**9. RealTrends / T3 Sixty Rankings**
Search "RealTrends [agent/team name]" or "[team name] RealTrends 500":
- If they appear on RealTrends 500, America's Best, or any ranking: significant production volume (250+ transactions typically required)
- T3 Sixty Mega Teams: 500+ transactions/year
- Being ranked = they care about recognition and status = use this in outreach

**10. Coaching / Conference Signals**
- "Tom Ferry" or "TF" mentioned: premium coaching client, significant marketing budget, likely $500K+ GCI
- "KW MAPS" or "Buffini" or "Mike Ferry" mentioned: systems-oriented, disciplined
- Presenting at KW Family Reunion, Inman Connect, NAR: thought leader, peer credibility is key
- "Mastermind" mentioned: high-performing peer group, high production

## Phase 2: The 8 Research Dimensions (Real Estate Edition)

### Dimension 1: Agent/Team Overview

| Recognition / rankings | RealTrends, local biz awards | RealTrends search, website |
| Coaching / associations | Tom Ferry, NAR member, local board | Website, social bios |

### Dimension 2: Production Volume (Revenue Equivalent)

**Data points to capture:**

| Field | Description | Sources |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| Transactions / year | Closed deals in last 12 months (estimate) | Zillow sold count, Realtor.com |
| Average sale price | Median sold listing price | Zillow recent sales |
| Total sales volume | Transactions × avg price | Calculated |
| GCI estimate | Volume × 2.5% commission rate | Calculated (flag as estimate) |
| Buyer-side vs. listing-side ratio | Which side do they dominate | Review content, recent sales |
| Price range | Entry-level / move-up / luxury | Zillow recent sales |
| Market share in farm | Approximate % of zip code sales | Zillow data + ZIP volume estimates |
| Trend | Growing / stable / declining production | Year-over-year Zillow data if available |

**GCI estimation table:**
| Annual Transactions | Avg Sale Price | Est. GCI |
|--------------------|----------------|---------|
| 10–24 | $300K | $75K–$180K |
| 25–50 | $400K | $250K–$500K |
| 50–100 | $450K | $562K–$1.1M |
| 100–250 | $500K | $1.25M–$3.1M |
| 250+ | $500K+ | $3.1M+ |

Always state: "GCI estimated at approximately $[X] based on [X] recent Zillow/Realtor.com sold listings at average $[Y] sale price × 2.5% commission. Treat as directional — not confirmed."

### Dimension 3: Technology Stack

**Data points to capture:**

| Technology Category | Tool Detected | Detection Method | Sales Implication |
|--------------------|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| CRM / Lead Nurture | BoomTown / FUB / kvCORE / Chime | Job postings, website footer, chatbot branding | Incumbent system — displacement play or integration pitch |
| IDX Website | Real Geeks / Placester / Luxury Presence | Source code, copyright footer | Website quality and lead capture effectiveness |
| Transaction Management | Dotloop / Skyslope / DocuSign | Job posting requirements | Volume indicator; Dotloop = NAR member benefit |
| Video Email | BombBomb / Loom | Social media content format | Tech-forward agent — receptive to new tools |
| Dialer | Mojo / RedX / Vulcan7 | Job postings (ISA required tools) | Prospecting-heavy operation |
| Listing Marketing | Matterport / HomeJab / BoxBrownie | Virtual tour links on listings | Invested in listing presentation quality |
| Marketing Automation | kvCORE integrated / MailChimp | Website footer, job postings | Automated nurture in place or not |
| CMA Tool | Cloud CMA / RPR / HomeBot | Not usually visible externally | Rarely detectable — ask in discovery |
| Showing Software | ShowingTime / Calendly | Listing syndication links | Operational sophistication |

**Tech Sophistication Rating:**
- **High (≥4 tools detected):** Tech-forward, open to new solutions, higher budget
- **Medium (2–3 tools):** Some systems in place, room for improvement
- **Low (0–1 tools):** Opportunity to introduce foundational tools — or risk aversion

**Acquisition channel dependency assessment:**
- Portal-heavy (Zillow/Realtor.com primary): Dependent on lead cost fluctuations; ROI-sensitive; actively looking to reduce portal dependency
- Social ads primary: Tech-forward; performance-marketing mindset; track KPIs
- Sphere/referral only: Traditional agent; may resist new tools but has loyal client base
- Diversified: Multiple channels active — most sophisticated profile

### Dimension 6: Pain Signals & Trigger Events

**Real estate-specific pain categories:**

| Pain Category | Detection Signal | Outreach Angle |
|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Lead conversion gap** | ISA job posting; high Zillow reviews mentioning "slow response" | "You're generating leads but not converting them at the rate the investment deserves" |
| **Team scaling pains** | Multiple agent/coordinator job postings simultaneously | "Growing the team creates coordination and follow-up gaps that cost production" |
| **Portal lead quality decline** | Reviews mention "Zillow leads aren't converting"; recent negative Zillow reviews | "Most teams your size are reducing portal dependency — here's what the transition looks like" |
| **Listing-to-close gap** | Reviews mention "missed follow-ups" or "communication issues" | "Transaction coordination breakdowns are the #1 team reputation risk" |
| **Database neglect** | No email marketing, no newsletter, no drip campaigns visible | "You have a database of past clients that isn't being marketed to — the cheapest leads you'll ever generate" |
| **Brokerage switch signal** | State license lookup shows recent brokerage change (< 6 months) | "Fresh start with a new brokerage is the right moment to rebuild your tech stack from scratch" |
| **Production plateau** | Same or declining Zillow transaction count year-over-year | "Growth plateaus at this production level almost always come down to lead gen channel mix and conversion" |
| **Injector dependency (listing agent)** | All reviews mention the same listing agent by name | "If your top producer left, what percentage of your listings go with them?" |

## Phase 3: Fit Scoring (Real Estate Edition)

### Prospect Fit Score (0-100)

| Dimension | Max Points | Real Estate-Specific Criteria |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| **Production Volume** | 25 | 20-25: 50+ transactions/year or $500K+ GCI est. 15-19: 25–50 tx. 10-14: 10–25 tx. 0-9: Under 10 tx (micro-agent, low budget signal) |
| **Tech Sophistication** | 20 | 17-20: 3+ tech tools detected, IDX website, active CRM confirmed. 12-16: 2 tools detected. 6-11: Website + email only. 0-5: No digital infrastructure detected |
| **Growth Trajectory** | 20 | 17-20: Hiring + expanding market + recent trigger event. 12-16: One growth signal (hiring or new market). 6-11: Stable. 0-5: Declining production or inactivity signals |
| **Pain Signal Strength** | 20 | 17-20: 2+ clear pain signals detected (ISA hiring + CRM mismatch, etc.). 12-16: 1 clear pain signal. 6-11: Inferred pain from production level. 0-5: No pain signals found |
| **Contact Reachability** | 15 | 13-15: Agent email + phone + social + active online presence. 9-12: Email + one social channel. 5-8: Website contact form only. 0-4: No contact info found |

**Score interpretation for real estate:**
- 80–100: Ideal prospect — all signals aligned. Prioritize immediately.
- 60–79: Strong prospect — 1-2 gaps. Research further or proceed with best available angle.
- 40–59: Moderate prospect — production signals are there but pain/contact is unclear.
- Under 40: Low priority — insufficient production or budget signals.
