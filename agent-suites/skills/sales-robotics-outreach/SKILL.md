## Phase 1: Automation-Specific Personalization Research

**CRITICAL RULE:** Never write a single email before completing research. VP Supply Chain executives receive hundreds of vendor emails per week. The only ones that get responses are those that reference a specific, verifiable fact about their operation — a job posting number, a facility announcement date, a trade publication mention. Generic automation pitches are deleted in under 2 seconds.

### 1.1 Trigger Research — Execute All Four

**Step 1: Indeed labor signal check (highest ROI trigger in this vertical)**
Search "[company name] warehouse" on indeed.com:
- Count of open picker/selector/fulfillment associate positions → number
- Sign-on bonus or premium pay language → signal of labor crisis severity
- Multiple simultaneous locations posting → network-level opportunity
- Hiring "Automation Engineer" or "Robotics Technician" → active automation evaluation

Document: "As of [date], [company] has [X] open warehouse positions on Indeed, including [specific position titles]. [If freezer premium / sign-on bonus: Note sign-on bonus of $[X] mentioned — indicates significant difficulty filling positions.]"

**Step 2: Greenfield / facility announcement check**
Search:
```
"[company name]" "distribution center" OR "new DC" OR "new facility" site:supplychaindive.com
"[company name]" "broke ground" OR "breaking ground" warehouse [current year]
"[company name]" "lease" OR "expansion" "[city]" distribution
```
If greenfield found: This is the hottest possible trigger — contact within 30 days of announcement.

**Step 3: Trade press check (DC Velocity, Supply Chain Dive, Modern Materials Handling)**
```
site:dcvelocity.com "[company name]"
site:supplychaindive.com "[company name]"
site:mmh.com "[company name]"
```
Look for: case studies featuring the company, executive quotes, conference presentations, automation mentions, network expansion announcements.

**Step 4: LinkedIn check for the specific executive**
For each decision maker identified:
- Recent posts (last 90 days): what supply chain topics are they engaging with?
- Conference presentations: MODEX, ProMat, WERC, CSCMP speaker?
- LinkedIn articles or shares mentioning automation, labor, throughput
- Company or role changes in last 6 months

## Phase 3: The 5-Email Warehouse Automation Sequence

### Automation Email Writing Rules

**Length:** Under 100 words for emails 1–3. Under 75 words for emails 4–5. VP Supply Chain reads email on mobile between warehouse walks and meetings.

**Tone rules:**
- Write as a supply chain peer, not a robotics vendor
- Speak to the business outcome (throughput, labor cost, fill rate), not the technology (AMRs, conveyors, robots)
- One number per email minimum — ROI estimates must be grounded in their sub-vertical reality
- Never start with "I hope you're well" / "My name is" / "I'm reaching out"
- Never say: "our solution," "our platform," "our technology," "our software," "industry-leading," "state-of-the-art," "cutting-edge"

**Do say:** "companies in [sub-vertical] at your throughput level," "the labor math at [X] picks/hour," "what a [3PL / food distributor / building materials DC] found after 90 days," "FEFO compliance without headcount increase," "payback in [X] years based on [specific assumption]"

**Subject lines that land in warehouse automation:**
- "your [X] open picker positions"
- "the new [city] DC"
- "what [sub-vertical peer] found at year 2"
- "store replenishment throughput question"
- "your Q4 labor plan"
- "e-commerce fulfillment and your DC in [city]"
- "FEFO compliance question"
- "the greenfield decision at [company]"

### Email 2 — The Value Add (Day 3)

**Goal:** Share one piece of supply chain intelligence they can use regardless of whether they respond. This email earns trust — it does not ask for a meeting.

**Automation value-add options:**
- WERC Benchmark: "WERC (Warehouse Education and Research Council) data shows the average warehouse picks 68 units/associate/hour manually. Top-performing automated operations achieve 175–250 units/equivalent labor hour. The 2× gap is almost entirely in pick path optimization and labor variability reduction."
- Labor deflation math: "The average warehouse turnover cost is $3,500–$5,000 per associate (recruiting, training, productivity ramp). At 80% annual turnover on 100 associates, that's $280,000–$400,000/year in labor churn cost alone — before the labor premium."
- Greenfield ROI framing: "Every $1M of ASRS/automation designed into a greenfield costs $1.8–$2.2M retrofitted into an existing facility. The design phase is the decision gate."
- FEFO/accuracy framing for F&B: "FDA FSMA Section 204 traceability requirements (effective Jan 2026) require lot-level tracking to the case. Manual lot rotation at high throughput has a documented FEFO compliance failure rate of 2–8%. Automated FEFO enforcement reduces this to under 0.1%."
- 3PL competitive framing: "3PLs with certified automated facilities win 22–28% more RFPs for e-commerce clients vs. manual operations, per MHI annual report. The automation investment becomes a top-line revenue driver, not just a cost reduction."

## Phase 4: Multi-Threading Strategy (Unique to This Vertical)

Warehouse automation is a committee decision — VP Supply Chain, Director of Engineering, and CFO all have veto power. Single-threaded outreach to one contact fails 80%+ of the time. This vertical requires 3-thread outreach from Day 1.

**Thread 1: VP Supply Chain / VP Operations (economic buyer)**
- Primary email sequence (all 5 emails above)
- Framing: business outcome + labor economics + competitive risk
- Best channel: direct email + LinkedIn

**Thread 2: Director of Engineering / Automation Manager (technical champion)**
- Parallel LinkedIn connection + email sequence
- Different framing: WMS integration specifics, system architecture, pilot program design
- Best channel: LinkedIn (this persona is more active here than VP Supply Chain)
- Email subject lines for this persona: "WMS integration for [Manhattan/Blue Yonder]," "[ASRS/AMR] technical specs for your [city] facility," "pilot program design — [sub-vertical] DC"

**Thread 3: CFO / VP Finance (financial gatekeeper — introduce at Email 4 stage)**
- Do NOT email CFO first — come through the operational sponsor with a warm introduction
- At Email 4 stage, if no response from VP Supply Chain, send CFO a shorter version of the ROI angle
- Subject: "the automation CapEx question for [company]" or "OpEx to CapEx — [city] DC model"
- Framing: payback period, NPV at 5 years, OpEx conversion narrative, IRA Section 45X tax credits

**Thread 4: DC General Manager (ground-level champion — LinkedIn only)**
- Follow on LinkedIn Day 0 after email sent to VP
- Engage with content (Day 5 comment on their post if they have one)
- LinkedIn message Day 10: "Hi [name] — connected with [VP name] about the throughput picture at [city]. Thought you'd find this [article / model / benchmark] useful as well."
- Goal: activate a bottom-up champion who validates the pain to the VP
