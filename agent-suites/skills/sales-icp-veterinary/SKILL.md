## Vertical Context

VERTICAL: Veterinary Practices
SUB-TYPES: General practice (companion animal), emergency & critical care (EC), specialty hospital (surgery, oncology, dermatology, ophthalmology, cardiology), mixed/large animal practice, mobile vet
BUYERS:
  - Economic Buyer: Practice Owner (DVM) or Hospital Director
  - Technical Buyer: Practice Manager / Hospital Administrator
  - Influencer: Lead Veterinary Technician, Receptionist Lead
  - Blocker: Corporate parent (VCA, Banfield/Mars, NVA, PetVet, BluePearl) — purchasing is centralized
REVENUE MODEL: Services (exam, diagnostics, surgery, hospitalization) + retail (Rx, prescription food, OTC products). No insurance billing (pet insurance is client-paid, not provider-billed). Cash/credit card dominant. Avg general practice revenue $800K–$3M. Emergency hospital $3M–$15M+.
PRACTICE MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE (PIMS): AVImark (legacy, largest install base), Cornerstone (IDEXX), ezyVet (cloud, fast-growing), Vetspire, Shepherd, ImproMed, VetLinker — PIMS integration is a hard purchase filter
TERMINOLOGY: PIMS (practice information management system), SOAPnote, wellness visit, compliance rate (heartworm/flea prevention), white-label Rx, in-house pharmacy, VIN (Veterinary Information Network), AVMA, DVM/VMD, CVT/RVT (registered vet tech), triage, hospitalized case, discharge instructions, reminder protocol
COMPLIANCE: DEA (Schedule II-V controlled substances), VCPR (Veterinarian-Client-Patient Relationship), state veterinary medical board, USDA accreditation (for export health certificates), OSHA
KEY PAIN POINTS:
  1. Vet tech and support staff shortage — turnover rate 30-40%, open positions unfilled for months
  2. Appointment backlog — new patient waits of 3-6 weeks are common post-COVID, leading to client frustration
  3. Pharmacy revenue leakage to Chewy/1-800-PetMeds — clients filling prescriptions online instead of in-clinic
  4. Client compliance rates — pets not returning for heartworm/flea prevention, vaccines, dental cleanings
  5. No-shows and last-minute cancellations disrupting workflow
  6. Fear of corporate consolidation — many independent owners are anxious about VCA/NVA buying them out
BUDGET SIGNALS: Multiple DVMs on staff, in-house diagnostics (IDEXX ProCyte, Catalyst analyzer, digital X-ray, ultrasound), specialty services (surgery, dermatology), after-hours or emergency services, online pharmacy partnership
SEASONAL: Heartworm/flea/tick season peaks spring-early summer (reminder campaigns). Holiday boarding season (Thanksgiving, Christmas). Wellness visit peaks spring/fall. Slow period: January-February (post-holiday).
DATA SOURCES: NPI Registry (taxonomy 174M00000X for veterinary), AVMA member directory, state VMB license lookup, Google Business Profile, Yelp, VetRatingz, IDEXX dealer relationships

**Critical distinction — Independent vs. Corporate:**
- Independent: Practice owner is the DVM. Decision in 1-4 weeks. High relationship value.
- Corporate (VCA, Banfield, NVA, PetVet, BluePearl): Hospital manager has NO purchasing authority. Must go through regional/corporate procurement. Budget cycle is annual. Win rate is low without a corporate champion.

### Dimension 3: Behavioral Indicators (Veterinary-Specific)

- AVMA Annual Convention (July): national conference

**Hiring signals that reveal pain:**

**Review signals:**
- Google rating under 4.2 → client communication or wait time issues

- Reviews mentioning "rude staff" or "rushed" → staffing and workflow stress

### Dimension 4: Pain Point Map (Veterinary-Specific)

**Pain 1 — Staff Shortage & Burnout (CRITICAL)**
- Severity: Critical — 87% of practices report difficulty hiring vet techs (AVMA data)
- How it manifests: DVMs performing tech duties, appointments stretched, staff working mandatory overtime
- Business impact: Each unfilled tech position costs $30-$50K in lost capacity and locum costs
- Current workaround: Paying premium wages, using temp agencies (Veterinary Staffing Solutions), burning out existing staff
- Your angle: Anything that reduces clicks-per-task, automates reminders, or eliminates repetitive admin saves the team hours per day

**Pain 2 — Pharmacy Revenue Leakage (HIGH)**
- Severity: High — practices lose 30-60% of pharmacy revenue to Chewy/1-800-PetMeds
- How it manifests: Client fills heartworm/flea/tick prevention online after getting the Rx at the clinic
- Business impact: A $2M practice can lose $150-$300K/year in pharmacy margin
- Current workaround: Matching Chewy prices at the counter (margin destruction), AutoShip programs
- Your angle: In-house online pharmacy, prescription management, or client retention tools that keep Rx in-house

**Pain 3 — Client Compliance & Recall (HIGH)**
- Severity: High — average compliance rate for recommended services is 40-60% (industry benchmark)
- How it manifests: Patients due for vaccines, dental cleanings, or recheck exams not returning
- Business impact: A 10% compliance improvement on a 3,000-patient practice = $80-$150K in additional annual revenue
- Current workaround: Paper postcard reminders (expensive, low response), manual phone calls nobody has time for

**Pain 4 — Appointment Backlog (HIGH)**
- Severity: High — 3-6 week new patient wait times are common; urgent cases strain the system
- How it manifests: New clients going to competitors or emergency hospitals for non-emergency issues
- Business impact: Each new client lost to a competitor is worth $800-$1,500 lifetime value
- Current workaround: Waitlists managed in notebooks, no automated fill-in for cancellations

**Pain 5 — Fear of Corporate Acquisition (MEDIUM-HIGH)**
- Severity: Emotionally very high — owners are anxious about their exit options and legacy
- How it manifests: Ambivalence about investing in the practice vs. preparing to sell
- Your angle: Tools that increase practice valuation (EBITDA-improving technology) are compelling

### Dimension 5: Budget Qualification (Veterinary-Specific)

- **Minimum viable practice revenue:** $800K/year
- **Technology budget:** Vet practices spend 2-4% of revenue on technology ($20K-$80K/year for $1M-$2M practice)
- **Deal size sweet spot:** $200-$1,500/month for SaaS; IDEXX bundles can push practices toward consolidation
- **Budget cycle:** Informal. Decisions are made when pain is acute. No formal fiscal calendar for most independents.
- **Budget authority:** Owner-DVM decides alone for under $5K/year commitments. Over that threshold: discussion with practice manager and sometimes a spouse/business partner.
- **ROI language:** Appointments per day, revenue per DVM per day, client retention rate, compliance rate, pharmacy revenue recaptured — always connect to dollars not percentages alone

**External budget signals:**
- Recent diagnostic equipment purchase (ultrasound, digital X-ray) → capital appetite
- Multiple DVMs on website → payroll confidence = revenue confidence
- Running Facebook/Instagram ads → marketing budget exists
- Active online booking tool → already paying for tech

### Dimension 6: Channel Preferences (Veterinary-Specific)

**Ranked by effectiveness:**
1. **DVM peer referral** — highest trust signal. "My colleague Dr. Smith at Valley Vet uses it" closes deals.
2. **Veterinary conference exhibit hall** — VMX (January), AVMA (July), WVC — high-intent evaluation environment
3. **VHMA chapter meetings** — practice managers are the day-to-day champions
4. **VIN or Facebook Group posts** — DVMs trust forum peer recommendations (Veterinary Entrepreneurs, Vet Owners Facebook Group)
5. **Cold email to practice owner** — works if specific, short, and references a real DVM pain. Mobile-first.
6. **Cold call to practice manager** — higher answer rate. OM/PM can champion internally.
7. **Direct mail to clinic** — surprisingly effective; stands out from digital noise

**Content that converts vet buyers:**
- Real compliance rate or recall revenue numbers from a specific, named practice (with permission)
- Video testimonial from a DVM who gained back 5 hours/week — time savings resonate deeply
- Integration demo with their specific PIMS — show AVImark/Cornerstone/ezyVet sync live
- "We work with practices like yours" — specificity of "3-DVM suburban companion animal clinic" beats generic claims

## Negative ICP (Veterinary-Specific Disqualifiers)

1. **Corporate-owned (VCA, Banfield/Mars, NVA, PetVet, BluePearl)** — no local purchasing authority, long procurement cycle, low win rate
2. **Large-animal or equine-only practice** — completely different workflow, PIMS, and buyer psychology
3. **Practice with under $700K annual revenue** — price-sensitive, margins thin
4. **Owner who is within 3 years of selling to corporate** — actively shopping to acquisition groups, not investing in new tools
5. **Practices exclusively using IDEXX Cornerstone** — IDEXX bundles services and actively blocks third-party integrations; hard to displace
6. **Emergency-only hospital without general practice** — very different workflow and buyer (hospital director, not owner-DVM)
7. **Practice with no PIMS or paper-only records** — too early in digital maturity, not ready for add-on tools
8. **Solo DVM with no support staff** — no capacity to implement or champion anything new
9. **Practice currently in a lawsuit or under VMB investigation** — not making any non-essential decisions
10. **Franchise or corporate-affiliated brand (Petco Vet, PetSmart Banfield)** — retail-embedded, corporate purchasing only

## Buyer Personas (Veterinary-Specific)

### Persona 1: "The Mission-Driven Owner DVM"
- **Title:** Owner / General Practitioner DVM, age 38-55
- **Day-in-the-life:** Seeing patients 8am-6pm, managing staff crises between appointments, reviewing financials on weekends. Became a vet to help animals, not to run a business — but now the business runs her.
- **Goals/KPIs:** Revenue per DVM per day ($1,500-$2,500 target), client retention rate, staff retention (turnover is personal failure to her), patient compliance rates
- **Top frustrations:** Can't hire or keep good techs. Losing pharmacy revenue to Chewy. Clients not complying with recommended care. Feels like she's drowning in admin.
- **Information diet:** VIN forums, The Veterinary Podcast, AVMA news, Facebook groups (Vet Entrepreneurs Network)
- **Objections:** "We're already slammed — I can't add one more thing." "Does this work with AVImark?" "I've been burned by software promises before." "My tech team barely has time to breathe."
- **Messaging:** "This saves your team 45 minutes per day in reminder calls they're currently doing manually." "Your techs spend X hours/week on tasks this automates." "Here's what Dr. [name] at [nearby clinic] found after 90 days."
- **What turns them off:** Being told to "leverage AI" without specifics. Anything that sounds like more training. Vendors who don't understand VCPR or PIMS workflows.

### Persona 2: "The Practice Manager Champion"
- **Title:** Practice Manager / Hospital Administrator, age 30-48
- **Day-in-the-life:** Manages scheduling, inventory, staff scheduling, client complaints, and vendor relationships. The DVM trusts her judgment on operations and technology.
- **Goals/KPIs:** Schedule utilization, staff satisfaction scores, client wait times, pharmacy revenue, reminder compliance
- **Objections:** "I need the doctor to approve this." "Will this integrate with Cornerstone without breaking anything?" "We just switched PIMS last year — the team is exhausted."
- **Messaging:** "You'll have a single dashboard instead of three tabs open." "Setup takes 2 hours of your time, we handle everything else." "Here's the implementation timeline — week by week."
- **What turns them off:** Vendors who go around her to the DVM. Integration promises that aren't backed by documentation. Anything requiring IT support she doesn't have.

### Persona 3: "The Specialist Hospital Director"
- **Title:** Medical Director / Hospital Director (DVM), multi-DVM specialty hospital, age 40-55
- **Day-in-the-life:** Less clinical, more operational. Reviews KPIs across departments (surgery, oncology, ER). Manages referring DVM relationships — specialty hospitals live and die by GP referrals.
- **Goals/KPIs:** Case volume by specialty, referring DVM relationship count, ER utilization, staff-to-case ratio
- **Objections:** "We're different from a general practice — our workflow is more complex." "Show me how this works for specialty/emergency specifically." "This needs to go through our hospital administrator."
- **Messaging:** "We work with specialty and emergency hospitals specifically — here's what BluePearl Alternatives [an independent specialty group] saw." "Referral relationship management built for specialists, not general practice."

## Prospecting Playbook (Veterinary-Specific)

**Where to find veterinary practices:**
- **NPI Registry** (nppes.cms.gov): Taxonomy 174M00000X = veterinarians. Filter by state + city.
- **AVMA member directory** (avma.org): Search by state, find practice name and DVM
- **Google Maps:** Search "veterinarian [city]" — review count and rating are instant pain proxies
- **VetRatingz / Yelp:** Find practices with review complaints about scheduling or wait times
- **Indeed / ZipRecruiter:** Practices hiring multiple vet techs simultaneously = understaffed = pain

**LinkedIn search strings:**
```
"veterinary practice owner" AND "[city]"
"DVM" AND "owner" AND "veterinary" AND "[state]"
"practice manager" AND "veterinary" AND "[city]"
"veterinary hospital manager" AND "[state]"
```

**Timing tactics:**
- Best window: January-February (post-VMX conference, practice owners in evaluation mode)
- Second best: September-October (pre-heartworm season planning, post-summer recovery)
- Avoid: Holiday weeks (Thanksgiving, Christmas), peak summer (overwhelmed with appointments)
- Best outreach time: 7-8am (before clinic opens) or 6:30-7:30pm (after last appointment)

**First 3 disqualification checks:**
1. Does the website say "A VCA/NVA/Banfield/PetVet hospital"? (Yes = corporate, different motion)
2. Does the Google listing show "Closed — moving to new location" or similar? (In transition)
3. Is the only DVM listed a solo practitioner with no support staff listed? (Too small, too busy)

## Competitive Context (Veterinary-Specific)

**Category competitors:**
- **Client communication/recall:** PetDesk, VitusVet, Vetstoria, Vet2Pet, Weave (cross-vertical)
- **Online pharmacy:** Covetrus, Vetsource, Henry Schein Vet (VetSource white-label)
- **PIMS-native:** AVImark reminders, Cornerstone reminders — "free" but weak
- **Telemedicine:** Anipanion, GuardianVets, Airvet

**Key displacement:** PetDesk is the most common incumbent. Often purchased but underutilized because adoption stalls at the front desk. Underutilization = your opening.

**Market trend:** Corporate consolidation continues — independent practice owners who want to compete with VCA/Banfield need tools to match the patient experience of corporate chains without giving up their independence. Frame your solution as "compete with corporate without becoming corporate."
